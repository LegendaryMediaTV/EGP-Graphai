## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> <sup>a</sup>

Love righteousness, all you who are judges of the earth.<br>Think of the Lord<sup>b</sup> with a good mind.<br>Seek him in singleness of heart,<br>
<sup>2</sup> because he is found by those who don’t put him to the test,<br>and is manifested to those who trust him.<br>
<sup>3</sup> for crooked thoughts separate from God.<br>His Power convicts when it is tested,<br>and exposes the foolish;<br>
<sup>4</sup> because wisdom will not enter into a soul that devises evil,<br>nor dwell in a body that is enslaved by sin.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For a holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit,<br>and will depart from thoughts that are without understanding,<br>and will be ashamed when unrighteousness has come in.

<sup>6</sup> For<sup>c</sup> wisdom is a spirit who loves man,<br>and she will not hold a<sup>d</sup> blasphemer guiltless for his lips,<br>because God is witness of his inmost self,<br>and is a true overseer of his heart,<br>and a hearer of his tongue.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Because the spirit of the Lord has filled<sup>e</sup> the world,<br>and that which holds all things together knows what is said.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Therefore no one who utters unrighteous things will be unseen;<br>neither will Justice, when it convicts, pass him by.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For in his counsels the ungodly will be searched out,<br>and the sound of his words will come to the Lord to bring his lawless deeds to conviction;<br>
<sup>10</sup> because a jealous ear listens to all things,<br>and the noise of murmurings is not hidden.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Beware then of unprofitable murmuring,<br>and keep your tongue from slander;<br>because no secret utterance will go on its way void,<br>and a lying mouth destroys a soul.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Don’t court death in the error of your life.<br>Don’t draw destruction upon yourselves by the works of your hands;<br>
<sup>13</sup> because God didn’t make death,<br>neither does he delight when the living perish.<br>
<sup>14</sup> For he created all things that they might have being.<br>The generative powers of the world are wholesome,<br>and there is no poison of destruction in them,<br>nor has Hades<sup>f</sup> royal dominion upon earth;<br>
<sup>15</sup> for righteousness is immortal,<br>
<sup>16</sup> but ungodly men by their hands and their words summon death;<br>deeming him a friend they<sup>g</sup> pined away.<br>They made a covenant with him,<br>because they are worthy to belong with him.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. The _Wisdom of Solomon_ is recognized as Deuterocanonical Scripture by the Roman Catholic, Greek Orthodox, and Russian Orthodox Churches.
> - <sup>b</sup> 1. Gr. _in goodness._
> - <sup>c</sup> 6. Some authorities read _the spirit of wisdom is loving to man._
> - <sup>d</sup> 6. Or, _reviler_
> - <sup>e</sup> 7. Gr. _the inhabited earth._
> - <sup>f</sup> 14. Or, _a royal house_
> - <sup>g</sup> 16. Or, _were consumed_ with love of him

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> For they said<sup>a</sup> within themselves, with unsound reasoning,<br>“Our life is short and sorrowful.<br>There is no healing when a man comes to his end,<br>and no one was ever known who<sup>b</sup> was released from Hades.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Because we were born by mere chance,<br>and hereafter we will be as though we had never been,<br>because the breath in our nostrils is smoke,<br>and reason is a spark kindled by the beating of our heart,<br>
<sup>3</sup> which being extinguished, the body will be turned into ashes,<br>and the spirit will be dispersed as thin air.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Our name will be forgotten in time.<br>No one will remember our works.<br>Our life will pass away as the traces of a cloud,<br>and will be scattered as is a mist,<br>when it is chased by the rays of the sun,<br>and<sup>c</sup> overcome by its heat.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For our allotted time is the passing of a shadow,<br>and our end doesn’t retreat,<br>because it is securely sealed, and no one<sup>d</sup> turns it back.

<sup>6</sup> “Come therefore and let’s enjoy the good things that exist.<br>Let’s use the creation earnestly as in our youth.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Let’s fill ourselves with costly wine and perfumes,<br>and let no spring flower pass us by.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Let’s crown ourselves with rosebuds before they wither.<br>
<sup>9</sup> Let none of us go without his share in our proud revelry.<br>Let’s leave tokens of mirth everywhere,<br>because this is our portion, and this is our lot.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Let’s oppress the righteous poor.<br>Let’s not spare the widow,<br>nor regard the gray hair of the old man.<br>
<sup>11</sup> But let our strength be a law of righteousness;<br>for that which is weak is proven useless.<br>
<sup>12</sup> But let’s lie in wait for the righteous man,<br>because he annoys us,<br>is contrary to our works,<br>reproaches us with sins against the law,<br>and charges us with sins against our training.<br>
<sup>13</sup> He professes to have knowledge of God,<br>and calls himself a child of the Lord.<br>
<sup>14</sup> He became to us a reproof of our thoughts.<br>
<sup>15</sup> He is grievous to us even to look at,<br>because his life is unlike other men’s,<br>and his paths are strange.<br>
<sup>16</sup> We were regarded by him as something worthless,<br>and he abstains from our ways as from uncleanness.<br>He calls the latter end of the righteous happy.<br>He boasts that God is his father.<br>
<sup>17</sup> Let’s see if his words are true.<br>Let’s test what will happen at the end of his life.<br>
<sup>18</sup> For if the righteous man is God’s son, he will uphold him,<br>and he will deliver him out of the hand of his adversaries.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Let’s test him with insult and torture,<br>that we may find out how gentle he is,<br>and test his patience.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Let’s condemn him to a shameful death,<br>for he will be protected, according to his words.”

<sup>21</sup> Thus they reasoned, and they were led astray;<br>for their wickedness blinded them,<br>
<sup>22</sup> and they didn’t know the mysteries of God,<br>neither did they hope for wages of holiness,<br>nor did they discern that there is a prize for blameless souls.<br>
<sup>23</sup> Because God created man for incorruption,<br>and made him an image of his own everlastingness;<br>
<sup>24</sup> but death entered into the world by the envy of the devil,<br>and those who belong to him experience it.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. Or, _among_
> - <sup>b</sup> 1. Or, _returned out of Hades_
> - <sup>c</sup> 4. Gr. _weighed down._
> - <sup>d</sup> 5. Or, _comes again_

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God,<br>and no torment will touch them.<br>
<sup>2</sup> In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died.<br>Their departure was considered a disaster,<br>
<sup>3</sup> and their travel away from us ruin,<br>but they are in peace.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For even if in the sight of men they are punished,<br>their hope is full of immortality.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Having borne a little chastening, they will receive great good;<br>because God tested them, and found them worthy of himself.<br>
<sup>6</sup> He tested them like gold in the furnace,<br>and he accepted them as a whole burnt offering.<br>
<sup>7</sup> In the time of their visitation they will shine.<br>They will run back and forth like sparks among stubble.<br>
<sup>8</sup> They will judge nations and have dominion over peoples.<br>The Lord will reign over them forever.<br>
<sup>9</sup> Those who trust him will understand truth.<br>The faithful will live with him in love,<br>because grace and mercy are with his chosen ones.

<sup>10</sup> But the ungodly will be punished even as their reasoning deserves,<br>those who neglected righteousness and revolted from the Lord;<br>
<sup>11</sup> for he who despises wisdom and discipline is miserable.<br>Their hope is void and their toils unprofitable.<br>Their works are useless.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Their wives are foolish and their children are wicked.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Their descendants are cursed.<br>For the barren woman who is undefiled is happy,<br>she who has not conceived in transgression.<br>She will have fruit when God examines souls.<br>
<sup>14</sup> So is the eunuch which has done no lawless deed with his hands,<br>nor imagined wicked things against the Lord;<br>for a precious gift will be given to him for his faithfulness,<br>and a delightful inheritance in the Lord’s sanctuary.<br>
<sup>15</sup> For good labors have fruit of great renown.<br>The root of understanding can’t fail.<br>
<sup>16</sup> But children of adulterers will not come to maturity.<br>The seed of an unlawful union will vanish away.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For if they live long, they will not be esteemed,<br>and in the end, their old age will be without honor.<br>
<sup>18</sup> If they die young, they will have no hope,<br>nor consolation in the day of judgment.<br>
<sup>19</sup> For the end of an unrighteous generation is always grievous.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> It is better to be childless with virtue,<br>for immortality is in the memory of virtue,<br>because it is recognized both before God and before men.<br>
<sup>2</sup> When it is present, people imitate it.<br>They long after it when it has departed.<br>Throughout all time it marches, crowned in triumph,<br>victorious in the competition for the prizes that are undefiled.<br>
<sup>3</sup> But the multiplying brood of the ungodly will be of no profit,<br>and their illegitimate offshoots won’t take deep root,<br>nor will they establish a sure hold.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For even if they grow branches and flourish for a season,<br>standing unsure, they will be shaken by the wind.<br>They will be uprooted by the violence of winds.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Their branches will be broken off before they come to maturity.<br>Their fruit will be useless,<br>never ripe to eat, and fit for nothing.<br>
<sup>6</sup> For unlawfully conceived children are witnesses of wickedness<br>against parents when they are investigated.

<sup>7</sup> But a righteous man, even if he dies before his time, will be at rest.<br>
<sup>8</sup> For honorable old age is not that which stands in length of time,<br>nor is its measure given by number of years,<br>
<sup>9</sup> but understanding is gray hair to men,<br>and an unspotted life is ripe old age.

<sup>10</sup> Being found well-pleasing to God, someone was loved.<br>While living among sinners he was transported.<br>
<sup>11</sup> He was caught away, lest evil should change his understanding,<br>or guile deceive his soul.<br>
<sup>12</sup> For the fascination of wickedness obscures the things which are good,<br>and the whirl of desire perverts an innocent mind.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Being made perfect quickly,<br>he filled a long time;<br>
<sup>14</sup> for his soul was pleasing to the Lord.<br>Therefore he hurried out of the midst of wickedness.<br>
<sup>15</sup> But the peoples saw and didn’t understand,<br>not considering this, that grace and mercy are with his chosen,<br>and that he visits his holy ones;<br>
<sup>16</sup> but a righteous man who is dead will condemn the ungodly who are living,<br>and youth who is quickly perfected will condemn the many years of an unrighteous man’s old age.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For the ungodly will see a wise man’s end,<br>and won’t understand what the Lord planned for him,<br>and why he safely kept him.<br>
<sup>18</sup> They will see, and they will despise;<br>but the Lord will laugh them to scorn.<br>After this, they will become a dishonored carcass<br>and a reproach among the dead forever;<br>
<sup>19</sup> because he will dash them speechless to the ground,<br>and will shake them from the foundations.<br>They will lie utterly waste.<br>They will be in anguish<br>and their memory will perish.

<sup>20</sup> They will come with coward fear when their sins are counted.<br>Their lawless deeds will convict them to their face.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Then the righteous man will stand in great boldness<br>before the face of those who afflicted him,<br>and those who make his labors of no account.<br>
<sup>2</sup> When they see him, they will be troubled with terrible fear,<br>and will be amazed at the marvel of salvation.<br>
<sup>3</sup> They will speak among themselves repenting,<br>and for distress of spirit they will groan,<br>“This was he whom we used to hold in derision,<br>as a parable of reproach.<br>
<sup>4</sup> We fools considered his life madness,<br>and his end without honor.<br>
<sup>5</sup> How was he counted among sons of God?<br>How is his lot among saints?<br>
<sup>6</sup> Truly we went astray from the way of truth.<br>The light of righteousness didn’t shine for us.<br>The sun didn’t rise for us.<br>
<sup>7</sup> We<sup>a</sup> took our fill of the paths of lawlessness and destruction.<br>We traveled through trackless deserts,<br>but we didn’t know the Lord’s way.<br>
<sup>8</sup> What did our arrogance profit us?<br>What good have riches and boasting brought us?

<sup>9</sup> Those things all passed away as a shadow,<br>like a rumor that runs by,<br>
<sup>10</sup> like a ship passing through the billowy water,<br>which, when it has gone by, there is no trace to be found,<br>no pathway of its keel in the waves.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Or it is like when a bird flies through the air,<br>no evidence of its passage is found,<br>but the light wind, lashed with the stroke of its pinions,<br>and torn apart with the violent rush of the moving wings, is passed through.<br>Afterwards no sign of its coming remains.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Or it is like when an arrow is shot at a mark,<br>the air it divided closes up again immediately,<br>so that men don’t know where it passed through.<br>
<sup>13</sup> So we also, as soon as we were born, ceased to be;<br>and we had no sign of virtue to show,<br>but we were utterly consumed in our wickedness.”<br>
<sup>14</sup> Because the hope of the ungodly man is like chaff carried by the wind,<br>and<sup>b</sup> as<sup>c</sup> foam vanishing before a tempest;<br>and is scattered like smoke by the wind,<br>and passes by as the remembrance of a guest who stays just a day.

<sup>15</sup> But the righteous live forever.<br>Their reward is in the Lord,<br>and the care for them with the Most High.<br>
<sup>16</sup> Therefore they will receive the crown of royal dignity<br>and the diadem of beauty from the Lord’s hand,<br>because he will cover them with his right hand,<br>and he will shield them with his arm.<br>
<sup>17</sup> He will take his zeal as complete armor,<br>and will make the whole creation his weapons to punish his enemies:<br>
<sup>18</sup> He will put on righteousness as a breastplate,<br>and will wear impartial judgment as a helmet.<br>
<sup>19</sup> He will take holiness as an invincible shield.<br>
<sup>20</sup> He will sharpen stern wrath for a sword.<br>The universe will go with him to fight against his frenzied foes.<br>
<sup>21</sup> Shafts of lightning will fly with true aim.<br>They will leap to the mark from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow.<br>
<sup>22</sup> Hailstones full of wrath will be hurled as from a catapult.<br>The water of the sea will be angered against them.<br>Rivers will sternly overwhelm them.<br>
<sup>23</sup> A mighty wind will encounter them.<br>It will winnow them away like a tempest.<br>So lawlessness will make all the land desolate.<br>Their evil-doing will overturn the thrones of princes.

> - <sup>a</sup> 7. See Proverbs 14:14
> - <sup>b</sup> 14. Gr. _like foam chased to thinness:_ or, _as thin foam chased._
> - <sup>c</sup> 14. Most Greek authorities read _hoar frost:_ some authorities, perhaps rightly, _a spider’s web._

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> Hear therefore, you kings, and understand.<br>Learn, you judges of the ends of the earth.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Give ear, you rulers who have dominion over many people,<br>and make your boast<sup>a</sup> in multitudes of nations,<br>
<sup>3</sup> because your dominion was given to you from the Lord,<br>and your sovereignty from the Most High.<br>He will search out your works,<br>and will inquire about your plans,<br>
<sup>4</sup> because being officers of his kingdom, you didn’t judge rightly,<br>nor did you keep the law,<br>nor did you walk according to God’s counsel.<br>
<sup>5</sup> He will come upon you awfully and swiftly,<br>because a stern judgment comes on those who are in high places.<br>
<sup>6</sup> For the man of low estate may be pardoned in mercy,<br>but mighty men will be mightily tested.<br>
<sup>7</sup> For the Sovereign Lord of all will not be impressed with anyone,<br>neither will he show deference to greatness;<br>because it is he who made both small and great,<br>and cares about them all;<br>
<sup>8</sup> but the scrutiny that comes upon the powerful is strict.<br>
<sup>9</sup> Therefore, my words are to you, O princes,<br>that you may learn wisdom and not fall away.<br>
<sup>10</sup> For those who have kept the things that are holy in holiness will be made holy.<br>Those who have been taught them will find what to say in defense.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Therefore set your desire on my words.<br>Long for them, and you princes will be instructed.

<sup>12</sup> Wisdom is radiant and doesn’t fade away;<br>and is easily seen by those who love her,<br>and found by those who seek her.<br>
<sup>13</sup> She anticipates those who desire her, making herself known.<br>
<sup>14</sup> He who rises up early to seek her won’t have difficulty,<br>for he will find her sitting at his gates.<br>
<sup>15</sup> For to think upon her is perfection of understanding,<br>and he who watches for her will quickly be free from care;<br>
<sup>16</sup> because she herself goes around, seeking those who are worthy of her,<br>and in their paths she appears to them graciously,<br>and in every purpose she meets them.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For her true beginning is desire for instruction;<br>and desire for instruction is love.<br>
<sup>18</sup> And love is observance of her laws.<br>To give heed to her laws confirms immortality.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Immortality brings closeness to God.<br>
<sup>20</sup> So then desire for wisdom promotes to a kingdom.

<sup>21</sup> If therefore you delight in thrones and sceptres, you princes of peoples,<br>honor wisdom, that you may reign forever.<br>
<sup>22</sup> But what wisdom is, and how she came into being, I will declare.<br>I won’t hide mysteries from you;<br>but I will explore from her first beginning,<br>bring the knowledge of her into clear light,<br>and I will not pass by the truth.<br>
<sup>23</sup> Indeed, I won’t travel with consuming envy,<br>because envy will have no fellowship with wisdom.<br>
<sup>24</sup> But a multitude of wise men is salvation to the world,<br>and an understanding king is stability for his people.<br>
<sup>25</sup> Therefore be instructed by my words, and you will profit.

> - <sup>a</sup> 2. Or, _in the multitudes of your nations_

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> I myself am also<sup>a</sup> mortal, like everyone else,<br>and am a descendant of one formed first and born of the earth.<br>
<sup>2</sup> I molded into flesh in the time of ten months in my mother’s womb,<br>being compacted in blood from the seed of man and pleasure of marriage.<br>
<sup>3</sup> I also, when I was born, drew in the common air,<br>and fell upon the kindred earth,<br>uttering, like all, for my first voice, the same cry.<br>
<sup>4</sup> I was nursed with care in swaddling clothes.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For no king had a different beginning,<br>
<sup>6</sup> but all men have one entrance into life, and a common departure.

<sup>7</sup> For this cause I prayed, and understanding was given to me.<br>I asked, and a spirit of wisdom came to me.<br>
<sup>8</sup> I preferred her before sceptres and thrones.<br>I considered riches nothing in comparison to her.<br>
<sup>9</sup> Neither did I liken to her any priceless gem,<br>because all gold in her presence is a little sand,<br>and silver will be considered as clay before her.<br>
<sup>10</sup> I loved her more than health and beauty,<br>and I chose to have her rather than light,<br>because her bright shining is never laid to sleep.<br>
<sup>11</sup> All good things came to me with her,<br>and innumerable riches are in her hands.<br>
<sup>12</sup> And I rejoiced over them all because wisdom leads them;<br>although I didn’t know that she was their mother.<br>
<sup>13</sup> As I learned without guile, I impart without grudging.<br>I don’t hide her riches.<br>
<sup>14</sup> For she is a treasure for men that doesn’t fail,<br>and those who use it obtain friendship with God,<br>commended by the gifts which they present through discipline.

<sup>15</sup> But may God grant that I may speak his judgment,<br>and to conceive thoughts worthy of what has been given me;<br>because he is one who guides even wisdom and who corrects the wise.<br>
<sup>16</sup> For both we and our words are in his hand,<br>with all understanding and skill in various crafts.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For he himself gave me an unerring knowledge of the things that are,<br>to know the structure of the universe and the operation of the elements;<br>
<sup>18</sup> the beginning, end, and middle of times;<br>the alternations of the solstices and the changes of seasons;<br>
<sup>19</sup> the circuits of years and the positions of stars;<br>
<sup>20</sup> the natures of living creatures and the raging of wild beasts;<br>the violence of<sup>b</sup> winds and the thoughts of men;<br>the diversities of plants and the virtues of roots.<br>
<sup>21</sup> All things that are either secret or manifest I learned,<br>
<sup>22</sup> for wisdom, that is the architect of all things, taught me.

For there is in her a spirit that is quick to understand, holy,<br>unique, manifold, subtle, freely moving, clear in utterance, unpolluted,<br>distinct, invulnerable, loving what is good, keen, unhindered,<br>
<sup>23</sup> beneficent, loving toward man, steadfast, sure, free from care, all-powerful, all-surveying,<br>and penetrating through all spirits that are quick to understand, pure, most subtle.<br>
<sup>24</sup> For wisdom is more mobile than any motion.<br>Yes, she pervades and penetrates all things by reason of her purity.<br>
<sup>25</sup> For she is a breath of the power of God,<br>and a pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty.<br>Therefore nothing defiled can find entrance into her.<br>
<sup>26</sup> For she is a reflection of everlasting light,<br>an unspotted mirror of the working of God,<br>and an image of his goodness.<br>
<sup>27</sup> Although she is one, she has power to do all things.<br>Remaining in herself, she renews all things.<br>From generation to generation passing into holy souls,<br>she makes friends of God and prophets.<br>
<sup>28</sup> For God loves nothing as much as one who dwells with wisdom.<br>
<sup>29</sup> For she is fairer than the sun,<br>and above all the constellations of the stars.<br>She is better than light.<br>
<sup>30</sup> For daylight yields to night,<br>but evil does not prevail against wisdom.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. Many authorities read _a mortal man._
> - <sup>b</sup> 20. Or, _spirits_

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> But she reaches from one end to the other with full strength,<br>and orders all things well.

<sup>2</sup> I loved her and sought her from my youth.<br>I sought to take her for my bride.<br>I became enamoured by her beauty.<br>
<sup>3</sup> She glorifies her noble birth by living with God.<br>The Sovereign Lord of all loves her.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For she is initiated into the knowledge of God,<br>and she chooses his works.<br>
<sup>5</sup> But if riches are a desired possession in life,<br>what is richer than wisdom, which makes all things?<br>
<sup>6</sup> And if understanding is effective,<br>who more than<sup>a</sup> wisdom is an architect of the things that exist?<br>
<sup>7</sup> If a man loves righteousness,<br>the fruits of wisdom’s labor<sup>b</sup> are virtues,<br>for she teaches soberness, understanding, righteousness, and courage.<br>There is nothing in life more profitable for people than these.<br>
<sup>8</sup> And if anyone longs for wide experience,<br>she knows the things of old, and infers the things to come.<br>She understands subtleties of speeches and interpretations of dark sayings.<br>She foresees signs and wonders, and the issues of seasons and times.

<sup>9</sup> Therefore I determined to take her to live with me,<br>knowing that she is one who would give me good counsel,<br>and encourage me in cares and grief.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Because of her, I will have glory among multitudes,<br>and honor in the sight of elders, though I am young.<br>
<sup>11</sup> I will be found keen when I give judgment.<br>I will be admired in the presence of rulers.<br>
<sup>12</sup> When I am silent, they will wait for me.<br>When I open my lips, they will heed what I say.<br>If I continue speaking, they will put their hands on their mouths.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Because of her, I will have immortality,<br>and leave behind an eternal memory to those who come after me.<br>
<sup>14</sup> I will govern peoples.<br>Nations will be subjected to me.<br>
<sup>15</sup> Dreaded monarchs will fear me when they hear of me.<br>Among the people, I will show myself to be good, and courageous in war.<br>
<sup>16</sup> When I come into my house, I will find rest with her.<br>For conversation with her has no bitterness,<br>and living with her has no pain, but gladness and joy.<br>
<sup>17</sup> When I considered these things in myself,<br>and thought in my heart how immortality is in kinship to wisdom,<br>
<sup>18</sup> and in her friendship is good delight,<br>and in the labors of her hands is wealth that doesn’t fail,<br>and understanding is in her companionship,<br>and great renown in having fellowship with her words,<br>I went about seeking how to take her to myself.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Now I was a clever child, and received a good soul.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Or rather, being good, I came into an undefiled body.<br>
<sup>21</sup> But perceiving that I could not otherwise possess wisdom unless God gave her to me—<br>yes, and to know and understand by whom the grace is given—<br>I pleaded with the Lord and implored him, and with my whole heart I said:

> - <sup>a</sup> 6. Gr. _she._
> - <sup>b</sup> 7. Gr. _her labors_

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> “O God of my ancestors and Lord of mercy,<br>who made all things by your word;<br>
<sup>2</sup> and by your wisdom you formed man,<br>that he should have dominion over the creatures that were made by you,<br>
<sup>3</sup> and rule the world in holiness and righteousness,<br>and execute judgment in uprightness of soul,<br>
<sup>4</sup> give me wisdom, her who sits by you on your thrones.<br>Don’t reject me from among your<sup>a</sup> servants,<br>

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> Wisdom prospered their works in the hand of a holy prophet.

<sup>3</sup> They withstood enemies and repelled foes.<br>
<sup>4</sup> They thirsted, and they called upon you,<br>and water was given to them out of the<sup>a</sup> flinty rock,<br>and healing of their thirst out of the hard stone.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For by what things their foes were punished,<br>by these they in their need were benefited.<br>
<sup>6</sup> When enemies were troubled with clotted blood<br>instead of a river’s ever-flowing fountain,<br>
<sup>7</sup> to rebuke the decree for the slaying of babies,<br>you gave them abundant water beyond all hope,<br>
<sup>8</sup> having shown by the thirst which they had suffered<br>how you punished the adversaries.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For when they were tried, although chastened in mercy,<br>they learned how the ungodly were tormented, being judged with wrath.<br>
<sup>10</sup> For you tested these as a father admonishing them;<br>but you searched out those as a stern king condemning them.<br>

<sup>14</sup> For him who long before was thrown out and exposed they stopped mocking.<br>In the end of what happened, they marveled,<br>having thirsted in another manner than the righteous.

<sup>19</sup> which had power not only to consume them by their violence,<br>but to destroy them even by the terror of their sight.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Yes and without these they might have fallen by a single breath,<br>being pursued by Justice, and scattered abroad by the breath of your power;<br>but you arranged all things by measure, number, and weight.

<sup>21</sup> For to be greatly strong is yours at all times.<br>Who could withstand the might of your arm?<br>

<sup>25</sup> How would anything have endured unless you had willed it?<br>Or that which was not called by you, how would it have been preserved?<br>
<sup>26</sup> But you spare all things, because they are yours,<br>O Sovereign Lord, you who love the living.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> For your incorruptible spirit is in all things.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Therefore you convict little by little those who fall from the right way,<br>and, putting them in remembrance by the things wherein they sin, you admonish them,<br>that escaping from their wickedness they may believe in you, O Lord.

<sup>3</sup> For truly the old inhabitants of your holy land,<br>
<sup>4</sup> hating them because they practiced detestable works of enchantments and unholy rites—<br>
<sup>5</sup> merciless slaughters of children<br>and sacrificial banquets of men’s flesh and of blood—<br>
<sup>6</sup> allies in an impious fellowship,<br>and murderers of their own helpless babes,<br>it was your counsel to destroy by the hands of our fathers;<br>

<sup>11</sup> For they were a cursed seed from the beginning.<br>It wasn’t through fear of any that you left them unpunished for their sins.

<sup>16</sup> For your strength is the source of righteousness,<br>and your sovereignty over all makes you to forbear all.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For when men don’t believe that you are perfect in power, you show your strength,<br>and in dealing with those who think this, you confuse their boldness.<br>
<sup>18</sup> But you, being sovereign in strength, judge in gentleness,<br>and with great forbearance you govern us;<br>for the power is yours whenever you desire it.

<sup>3</sup> If it was through delight in their beauty that they took them to be gods,<br>let them know how much better their Sovereign Lord is than these,<br>for the first author of beauty created them.<br>
<sup>4</sup> But if it was through astonishment at their power and influence,<br>then let them understand from them how much more powerful he who formed them is.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For from the greatness of the beauty of created things,<br>mankind forms the corresponding perception of their Maker.<sup>a</sup><br>
<sup>6</sup> But yet for these men there is but small blame,<br>for they too perhaps go astray<br>while they are seeking God and desiring to find him.<br>

<sup>10</sup> But they were miserable, and their hopes were in dead things,<br>who called them gods which are works of men’s hands,<br>gold and silver, skillfully made, and likenesses of animals,<br>or a useless stone, the work of an ancient hand.<br>

<sup>12</sup> Burning the scraps from his handiwork to cook his food,<br>he eats his fill.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Taking a discarded scrap which served no purpose,<br>a crooked piece of wood and full of knots,<br>he carves it with the diligence of his idleness,<br>and shapes it by the skill of his idleness.<br>He shapes it in the image of a man,<br>

<sup>15</sup> Having made a worthy chamber for it,<br>he sets it in a wall, securing it with iron.<br>
<sup>16</sup> He plans for it that it may not fall down,<br>knowing that it is unable to help itself<br>(for truly it is an image, and needs help).<br>
<sup>17</sup> When he makes his prayer concerning goods and his marriage and children,<br>he is not ashamed to speak to that which has no life.<br>
<sup>18</sup> Yes, for health, he calls upon that which is weak.<br>For life, he implores that which is dead.<br>For aid, he supplicates that which has no experience.<br>For a good journey, he asks that which can’t so much as move a step.<br>
<sup>19</sup> And for profit in business and good success of his hands,<br>he asks ability from that which has hands with no ability.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> For this cause, they were deservedly punished through creatures like those which they worship,<br>and tormented through a multitude of vermin.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Instead of this punishment, you, giving benefits to your people,<br>prepared quails for food,<br>a delicacy to satisfy the desire of their appetite,<br>
<sup>3</sup> to the end that your enemies, desiring food,<br>might for the hideousness of the creatures sent among them,<br>loathe even the necessary appetite;<br>but these, your people, having for a short time suffered lack,<br>might even partake of delicacies.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For it was necessary that inescapable lack should come upon those oppressors,<br>but that to these it should only be showed how their enemies were tormented.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For even when terrible raging of wild beasts came upon your people,<br>and they were perishing by the bites of crooked serpents,<br>your wrath didn’t continue to the uttermost;<br>
<sup>6</sup> but for admonition were they troubled for a short time,<br>having a token of salvation<br>to put them in remembrance of the commandment of your law;

<sup>7</sup> for he who turned toward it was not saved because of that which was seen,<br>but because of you, the Savior of all.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Yes, and in this you persuaded our enemies<br>that you are he who delivers out of every evil.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For the bites of locusts and flies truly killed them.<br>No healing for their life was found,<br>because they were worthy to be punished by such things.<br>
<sup>10</sup> But your children weren’t overcome by the very fangs of venomous dragons,<br>for your mercy passed by where they were and healed them.<br>
<sup>11</sup> For they were bitten to put them in remembrance of your oracles,<br>and were quickly saved, lest, falling into deep forgetfulness,<br>they should become unable to respond to your kindness.<br>
<sup>12</sup> For truly it was neither herb nor poultice that cured them,<br>but your word, O Lord, which heals all people.<br>
<sup>13</sup> For you have authority over life and death,<br>and you lead down to the gates of Hades, and lead up again.<br>
<sup>14</sup> But though a man kills by his wickedness,<br>he can’t retrieve the spirit that has departed<br>or release the imprisoned soul.

<sup>15</sup> But it is not possible to escape your hand;<br>
<sup>16</sup> for ungodly men, refusing to know you, were scourged in the strength of your arm,<br>pursued with strange rains and hails and relentless storms,<br>and utterly consumed with fire.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For, what was most marvelous,<br>in the water which quenches all things, the fire burned hotter;<br>for the world fights for the righteous.<br>
<sup>18</sup> For at one time the flame was restrained,<br>that it might not burn up the creatures sent against the ungodly,<br>but that these themselves as they looked might see that they were chased by the judgment of God.<br>
<sup>19</sup> At another time even in the midst of water it burns more intensely than fire,<br>that it may destroy the produce of an unrighteous land.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Instead of these things, you gave your people angels’ food to eat,<br>and you provided ready-to-eat bread for them from heaven without toil,<br>having the virtue of every pleasant flavor,<br>and agreeable to every taste.<br>
<sup>21</sup> For your nature showed your sweetness toward your children,<br>while that bread, serving the desire of the eater,<br>changed itself according to every man’s choice.<br>
<sup>22</sup> But snow and ice endured fire, and didn’t melt,<br>that people might know that fire was destroying the fruits of the enemies,<br>burning in the hail and flashing in the rains;<br>
<sup>23</sup> and that this fire, again, in order that righteous people may be nourished,<br>has even forgotten its own power.<br>
<sup>24</sup> For the creation, ministering to you, its maker,<br>strains its force against the unrighteous for punishment<br>and in kindness, slackens it on behalf of those who trust in you.<br>
<sup>25</sup> Therefore at that time also, converting itself into all forms,<br>it ministered to your all-nourishing bounty,<br>according to the desire of those who had need,<br>
<sup>26</sup> that your children, whom you loved, O Lord, might learn<br>that it is not the growth of crops that nourishes a man,<br>but that your word preserves those who trust you.<br>
<sup>27</sup> For that which was not destroyed by fire,<br>melted away when it was simply warmed by a faint sunbeam,<br>
<sup>28</sup> that it might be known that we must rise before the sun to give you thanks,<br>and must pray to you at the dawning of the light;<br>
<sup>29</sup> for the hope of the unthankful will melt as the winter’s hoar frost,<br>and will flow away as water that has no use.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> For your judgments are great, and hard to interpret;<br>therefore undisciplined souls went astray.<br>
<sup>2</sup> For when lawless men had supposed that they held a holy nation in their power,<br>they, prisoners of darkness, and bound in the fetters of a long night,<br>kept close beneath their roofs,<br>lay exiled from the eternal providence.<br>
<sup>3</sup> For while they thought that they were unseen in their secret sins,<br>they were divided from one another by a dark curtain of forgetfulness,<br>stricken with terrible awe, and very troubled by apparitions.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For neither did the dark recesses that held them guard them from fears,<br>but terrifying sounds rang around them,<br>and dismal phantoms appeared with unsmiling faces.<br>
<sup>5</sup> And no power of fire prevailed to give light,<br>neither were the brightest flames of the stars strong enough to illuminate that gloomy night;<br>
<sup>6</sup> but only the glimmering of a self-kindled fire appeared to them, full of fear.<br>In terror, they considered the things which they saw<br>to be worse than that sight, on which they could not gaze.<br>
<sup>7</sup> The mockeries of their magic arts were powerless, now,<br>and a shameful rebuke of their boasted understanding:<br>
<sup>8</sup> For those who promised to drive away terrors and disorders from a sick soul,<br>these were sick with a ludicrous fearfulness.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For even if no troubling thing frighted them,<br>yet, scared with the creeping of vermin and hissing of serpents,<br>
<sup>10</sup> they perished trembling in fear,<br>refusing even to look at the air, which could not be escaped on any side.<br>
<sup>11</sup> For wickedness, condemned by a witness within, is a coward thing,<br>and, being pressed hard by conscience, always has added forecasts of the worst.<br>
<sup>12</sup> For fear is nothing else but a surrender of the help which reason offers;<br>
<sup>13</sup> and from within, the expectation of being less<br>prefers ignorance of the cause that brings the torment.<br>
<sup>14</sup> But they, all through the night which was powerless indeed,<br>and which came upon them out of the recesses of powerless Hades,<br>sleeping the same sleep,<br>
<sup>15</sup> now were haunted by monstrous apparitions,<br>and now were paralyzed by their soul’s surrendering;<br>for sudden and unexpected fear came upon them.<br>
<sup>16</sup> So then whoever it might be, sinking down in his place,<br>was kept captive, shut up in that prison which was not barred with iron;<br>
<sup>17</sup> for whether he was a farmer, or a shepherd,<br>or a laborer whose toils were in the wilderness,<br>he was overtaken, and endured that inescapable sentence;<br>for they were all bound with one chain of darkness.<br>
<sup>18</sup> Whether there was a whistling wind,<br>or a melodious sound of birds among the spreading branches,<br>or a measured fall of water running violently,<br>
<sup>19</sup> or a harsh crashing of rocks hurled down,<br>or the swift course of animals bounding along unseen,<br>or the voice of wild beasts harshly roaring,<br>or an echo rebounding from the hollows of the mountains,<br>all these things paralyzed them with terror.<br>
<sup>20</sup> For the whole world was illuminated with clear light,<br>and was occupied with unhindered works,<br>
<sup>21</sup> while over them alone was spread a heavy night,<br>an image of the darkness that should afterward receive them;<br>but to themselves, they were heavier than darkness.

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> But for your holy ones there was great light.<br>Their enemies, hearing their voice but not seeing their form,<br>counted it a happy thing that they too had suffered,<br>
<sup>2</sup> yet for that they do not hurt them, though wronged by them before, they are thankful;<br>and because they had been at variance with them, they begged for pardon.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Therefore you provided a burning pillar of fire,<br>to be a guide for your people’s unknown journey,<br>and a harmless sun for their glorious exile.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For the Egyptians well deserved to be deprived of light and imprisoned by darkness,<br>they who had imprisoned your children,<br>through whom the incorruptible light of the law was to be given to the race of men.

<sup>5</sup> After they had taken counsel to kill the babes of the holy ones,<br>and when a single child had been abandoned and saved to convict them of their sin,<br>you took away from them their multitude of children,<br>and destroyed all their army together in a mighty flood.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Our fathers were made aware of that night beforehand,<br>that, having sure knowledge, they might be cheered by the oaths which they had trusted.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Salvation of the righteous and destruction of the enemies was expected by your people.<br>
<sup>8</sup> For as you took vengeance on the adversaries,<br>by the same means, calling us to yourself, you glorified us.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For holy children of good men offered sacrifice in secret,<br>and with one consent they agreed to the covenant of the divine law,<br>that they would partake alike in the same good things and the same perils,<br>the fathers already leading the sacred songs of praise.<br>
<sup>10</sup> But the discordant cry of the enemies echoed back,<br>and a pitiful voice of lamentation for children was spread abroad.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Both servant and master were punished with the same just doom,<br>and the commoner suffering the same as king;<br>
<sup>12</sup> Yes, they all together, under one form of death,<br>had corpses without number.<br>For the living were not sufficient even to bury them,<br>Since at a single stroke, their most cherished offspring was consumed.<br>
<sup>13</sup> For while they were disbelieving all things by reason of the enchantments,<br>upon the destruction of the firstborn they confessed the people to be God’s children.<br>
<sup>14</sup> For while peaceful silence wrapped all things,<br>and night in her own swiftness was half spent,<br>
<sup>15</sup> your all-powerful word leaped from heaven, from the royal throne,<br>a stern warrior, into the midst of the doomed land,<br>
<sup>16</sup> bearing as a sharp sword your authentic commandment,<br>and standing, it filled all things with death,<br>and while it touched the sky it stood upon the earth.<br>
<sup>17</sup> Then immediately apparitions in dreams terribly troubled them,<br>and unexpected fears came upon them.<br>
<sup>18</sup> And each, one thrown here half dead, another there,<br>made known why he was dying;<br>
<sup>19</sup> for the dreams, disturbing them, forewarned them of this,<br>that they might not perish without knowing why they were afflicted.

<sup>20</sup> The experience of death also touched the righteous,<br>and a multitude were destroyed in the wilderness,<br>but the wrath didn’t last long.<br>
<sup>21</sup> For a blameless man hurried to be their champion,<br>bringing the weapon of his own ministry,<br>prayer, and the atoning sacrifice of incense.<br>He withstood the indignation and set an end to the calamity,<br>showing that he was your servant.<br>
<sup>22</sup> And he overcame the anger,<br>not by strength of body, not by force of weapons,<br>but by his word, he subdued the avenger<br>by bringing to remembrance oaths and covenants made with the fathers.<br>
<sup>23</sup> For when the dead had already fallen in heaps one upon another,<br>he intervened and stopped the wrath,<br>and cut off its way to the living.<br>
<sup>24</sup> For the whole world was pictured on his long robe,<br>and the glories of the fathers were upon the engraving of the four rows of precious stones,<br>and your majesty was upon the diadem on his head.<br>
<sup>25</sup> The destroyer yielded to these, and they feared;<br>for it was enough only to test the wrath.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> But indignation without mercy came upon the ungodly to the end;<br>for God also foreknew their future,<br>
<sup>2</sup> how, having changed their minds to let your people go,<br>and having sped them eagerly on their way,<br>they would change their minds and pursue them.<br>
<sup>3</sup> For while they were yet in the midst of their mourning,<br>and lamenting at the graves of the dead,<br>they made another foolish decision,<br>and pursued as fugitives those whom they had begged to leave and driven out.<br>
<sup>4</sup> For the doom which they deserved was drawing them to this end,<br>and it made them forget the things that had happened to them,<br>that they might fill up the punishment which was yet lacking from their torments,<br>
<sup>5</sup> and that your people might journey on by a marvelous road,<br>but they themselves might find a strange death.

<sup>6</sup> For the whole creation, each part in its diverse kind, was made new again,<br>complying with your commandments,<br>that your servants might be kept unharmed.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Then the cloud that overshadowed the camp was seen,<br>and dry land rising up out of what had been water,<br>out of the Red sea an unhindered highway,<br>and a grassy plain out of the violent surge,<br>
<sup>8</sup> by which they passed over with all their army,<br>these who were covered with your hand,<br>having seen strange marvels.<br>
<sup>9</sup> For like horses they roamed at large,<br>and they skipped about like lambs,<br>praising you, O Lord, who was their deliverer.<br>
<sup>10</sup> For they still remembered the things that happened in the time of their sojourning,<br>how instead of bearing cattle, the land brought forth lice,<br>and instead of fish, the river spewed out a multitude of frogs.<br>
<sup>11</sup> But afterwards, they also saw a new kind of birds,<br>when, led on by desire, they asked for luxurious dainties;<br>
<sup>12</sup> for, to comfort them, quails came up for them from the sea.

<sup>13</sup> Punishments came upon the sinners,<br>not without the signs that were given beforehand by the violence of the thunder,<br>for they justly suffered through their own wickednesses,<br>for the hatred which they practiced toward guests was grievous indeed.<br>
<sup>14</sup> For while the others didn’t receive the strangers when they came to them,<br>the Egyptians made slaves of guests who were their benefactors.<br>
<sup>15</sup> And not only so, but while punishment of some sort will come upon the former,<br>since they received as enemies those who were aliens;<br>
<sup>16</sup> because these first welcomed with feastings,<br>and then afflicted with dreadful toils,<br>those who had already shared with them in the same rights.<br>
<sup>17</sup> And moreover they were stricken with loss of sight<br>(even as were those others at the righteous man’s doors),<br>when, being surrounded with yawning darkness,<br>they each looked for the passage through his own door.

<sup>18</sup> For as the notes of a lute vary the character of the rhythm,<br>even so the elements, changing their order one with another,<br>continuing always in its sound,<br>as may clearly be conjectured from the sight of the things that have happened.<br>
<sup>19</sup> For creatures of the dry land were turned into creatures of the waters,<br>and creatures that swim moved upon the land.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Fire kept the mastery of its own power in water,<br>and water forgot its quenching nature.<br>
<sup>21</sup> On the contrary, flames didn’t consume flesh of perishable creatures that walked among them,<br>neither did they melt the crystalline grains of ambrosial food that were melted easily.

<sup>22</sup> For in all things, O Lord, you magnified your people,<br>and you glorified them and didn’t lightly regard them,<br>standing by their side in every time and place.
