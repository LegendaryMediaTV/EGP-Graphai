## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> The Song of songs, which is Solomon’s.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth;<br>For thy love is better than wine.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Thine oils have a goodly fragrance;<br>Thy name is _as_ oil poured forth;<br>Therefore do the <sup>a</sup>virgins love thee.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Draw me; we will run after thee:<br>The king hath brought me into his chambers;<br>We will be glad and rejoice in thee;<br>We will make mention of thy love more than of wine:<sup>b</sup><br>Rightly do they love thee.<br>
<sup>5</sup> I am black, but comely,<br>O ye daughters of Jerusalem,<br>As the tents of Kedar,<br>As the curtains of Solomon.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Look not upon me, because I am swarthy,<br>Because the sun hath <sup>c</sup>scorched me.<br>My mother’s sons were incensed against me;<br>They made me keeper of the vineyards;<br>_But_ mine own vineyard have I not kept.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Tell me, O thou whom my soul loveth,<br>Where thou feedest _thy flock_,<br>Where thou makest _it_ to rest at noon:<br>For why should I be as one that is veiled<br>Beside the flocks of thy companions?<br>
<sup>8</sup> If thou know not, O thou fairest among women,<br>Go thy way forth by the footsteps of the flock,<br>And feed thy kids beside the shepherds’ tents.<br>
<sup>9</sup> I have compared thee, O <sup>d</sup>my love,<sup>e</sup><br>To a steed in Pharaoh’s chariots.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Thy cheeks are comely with plaits _of hair_,<br>Thy neck with strings of jewels.<br>
<sup>11</sup> We will make thee plaits of gold<br>With studs of silver.<br>
<sup>12</sup> While the king sat at his table,<br>My spikenard sent forth its fragrance.<br>
<sup>13</sup> My beloved is unto me _as_ a <sup>f</sup>bundle of myrrh,<br>That lieth betwixt my breasts.<br>
<sup>14</sup> My beloved is unto me _as_ a cluster of <sup>g</sup>henna-flowers<br>In the vineyards of En-gedi.<br>
<sup>15</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my love;<br>Behold thou art fair;<sup>h</sup><br>Thine eyes are _as_ doves.<br>
<sup>16</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my beloved, yea, pleasant:<br>Also our couch is green.<br>
<sup>17</sup> The beams of our <sup>i</sup>house are <sup>j</sup>cedars,<br>_And_ our rafters are <sup>k</sup>firs.<br>

## Chapter 2

<sup>2</sup> As a lily among thorns,<br>So is my love among the daughters.<br>
<sup>3</sup> As the apple-tree among the trees of the wood,<br>So is my beloved among the sons.<br>I <sup>c</sup>sat down under his shadow with great delight,<br>And his fruit was sweet to my taste.<br>
<sup>4</sup> He brought me to the <sup>d</sup>banqueting-house,<br>And his banner over me was love.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Stay ye me with <sup>e</sup>raisins, refresh me with apples;<br>For I am sick from love.<br>
<sup>6</sup> <sup>f</sup>His left hand _is_ under my head,<br>And his right hand doth embrace me.<br>
<sup>7</sup> I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>By the <sup>g</sup>roes, or by the hinds of the field,<br>That ye stir not up, nor awake _my_ love,<br>Until <sup>h</sup>he please.<br>
<sup>8</sup> The voice of my beloved! behold, he cometh,<br>Leaping upon the mountains,<br>Skipping upon the hills.<br>
<sup>9</sup> My beloved is like a <sup>i</sup>roe or a young hart:<br>Behold, he standeth behind our wall;<br>He looketh in at the windows;<br>He glanceth through the lattice.<br>
<sup>10</sup> My beloved spake, and said unto me,<br>Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away.<br>
<sup>11</sup> For, lo, the winter is past;<br>The rain is over and gone;<br>
<sup>12</sup> The flowers appear on the earth;<br>The time of the <sup>j</sup>singing _of birds_ is come,<br>And the voice of the turtle-dove is heard in our land;<br>
<sup>13</sup> The fig-tree ripeneth her green figs,<br>And the vines are in blossom;<br>They give forth their fragrance.<br>Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away.<br>
<sup>14</sup> O my dove, that art in the clefts of the rock,<br>In the covert of the steep place,<br>Let me see thy countenance,<br>Let me hear thy voice;<br>For sweet is thy voice, and thy countenance is comely.<br>
<sup>15</sup> Take us the foxes, the little foxes,<br>That spoil the vineyards;<br>For our vineyards are in blossom.<br>
<sup>16</sup> My beloved is mine, and I am his:<br>He feedeth _his flock_ among the lilies.<br>
<sup>17</sup> <sup>k</sup>Until the day <sup>l</sup>be cool, and the shadows flee away,<br>Turn, my beloved, and be thou like a <sup>m</sup>roe or a young hart<br>Upon the <sup>n</sup>mountains of <sup>o</sup>Bether.<br>

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> By night on my bed<br>I sought him whom my soul loveth:<br>I sought him, but I found him not.<br>
<sup>2</sup> _I said_, I will rise now, and go about the city;<br>In the streets and in the broad ways<br>I will seek him whom my soul loveth:<br>I sought him, but I found him not.<br>
<sup>3</sup> The watchmen that go about the city found me;<br>_To whom I said_, Saw ye him whom my soul loveth?<br>
<sup>4</sup> It was but a little that I passed from them,<br>When I found him whom my soul loveth:<br>I held him, and would not let him go,<br>Until I had brought him into my mother’s house,<br>And into the chamber of her that conceived me.<br>
<sup>5</sup> I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>By the roes, or by the hinds of the field,<br>That ye stir not up, nor awake _my_ love,<br>Until <sup>a</sup>he please.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness<br>Like pillars of smoke,<br>Perfumed with myrrh and frankincense,<br>With all powders of the merchant?<br>
<sup>7</sup> Behold, it is the litter of Solomon;<br>Threescore mighty men are about it,<br>Of the mighty men of Israel.<br>
<sup>8</sup> They all handle the sword, _and_ are expert in war:<br>Every man hath his sword upon his thigh,<br>Because of fear in the night.<br>
<sup>9</sup> King Solomon made himself a <sup>b</sup>palanquin<br>Of the wood of Lebanon.<br>
<sup>10</sup> He made the pillars thereof of silver,<br>The bottom thereof of gold, the seat of it of purple,<br>The midst thereof being <sup>c</sup>paved with love,<br>From the daughters of Jerusalem.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Go forth, O ye daughters of Zion, and behold king Solomon,<br>With the crown wherewith his mother hath crowned him<br>In the day of his espousals,<br>And in the day of the gladness of his heart.<br>

<sup>1</sup> Behold, thou art fair, my love; behold, thou art fair;<sup>a</sup><br>Thine eyes are _as_ doves behind thy <sup>b</sup>veil.<br>Thy hair is as a flock of goats,<br>That <sup>c</sup>lie along the side of mount Gilead.<br>

<sup>10</sup> How fair is thy love, my sister, _my_ bride!<br>How much better is thy love than wine!<br>And the fragrance of thine oils than all manner of spices!<br>
<sup>11</sup> Thy lips, O _my_ bride, <sup>m</sup>drop _as_ the honeycomb:<br>Honey and milk are under thy tongue;<br>And the smell of thy garments is like the smell of Lebanon.<br>
<sup>12</sup> A garden <sup>n</sup>shut up is my sister, _my_ bride;<br>A spring shut up, a fountain sealed.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Thy shoots are <sup>o</sup>an orchard of pomegranates, with precious fruits;<br>Henna with spikenard plants,<br>

<sup>15</sup> _Thou art_ a fountain of gardens,<br>A well of living waters,<br>And flowing streams from Lebanon.<br>
<sup>16</sup> Awake, O north wind; and come, thou south;<br>Blow upon my garden, that the spices thereof may flow out.<br>Let my beloved come into his garden,<br>And eat his precious fruits.<br>

<sup>16</sup> His <sup>s</sup>mouth is most sweet;<br>Yea, he is altogether lovely.<br>This is my beloved, and this is my friend,<br>O daughters of Jerusalem.<br>
