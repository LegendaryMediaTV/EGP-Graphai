## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> <sup>a</sup>Paul, a prisoner<sup>b</sup> of Christ Jesus,<sup>c</sup> and Timotheus the brother,<sup>d</sup> to Philemon<sup>e</sup> our beloved and fellow-worker,<sup>f</sup>
<sup>2</sup> and Apphia<sup>g</sup> the beloved, and Archippus our fellow-soldier, and the assembly in thy house:
<sup>3</sup> Grace<sup>h</sup> to you, and peace,<sup>i</sup> from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ!<sup>j</sup>

<sup>4</sup> I give thanks<sup>k</sup> to my God, always making mention of thee in my prayers,<sup>l</sup>
<sup>5</sup> hearing of thy love and faith that thou hast unto the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints,<sup>m</sup>
<sup>6</sup> that the fellowship of thy faith may become working in the full knowledge of every good thing that _is_ in you toward Christ Jesus;
<sup>7</sup> for we have much joy and comfort<sup>n</sup> in thy love, because the bowels of the saints have been refreshed through thee, brother.

<sup>8</sup> Wherefore, having in Christ much boldness<sup>o</sup> to command thee that which is fit—
<sup>9</sup> because of the love I rather entreat,<sup>p</sup> being such an one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ;
<sup>10</sup> I entreat thee concerning my child—whom I did beget in my bonds—Onesimus,<sup>q</sup>
<sup>11</sup> who once was to thee unprofitable, and now is profitable to me and to thee,
<sup>12</sup> whom I did send again, and thou him (that is, my own bowels) receive,
<sup>13</sup> whom I did wish to retain to myself, that in thy behalf he might minister<sup>r</sup> to me in the bonds of the good news,
<sup>14</sup> and apart from thy mind<sup>s</sup> I willed to do nothing, that as of necessity thy good deed may not be, but of willingness,
<sup>15</sup> for perhaps because of this he did depart<sup>t</sup> for an hour, that age-duringly thou mayest have him,<sup>u</sup>
<sup>16</sup> no more as a servant,<sup>v</sup> but above a servant—a brother beloved, especially to me, and how much more to thee, both in the flesh and in the Lord!
<sup>17</sup> If, then, with me thou hast fellowship, receive him as me,
<sup>18</sup> and if he did hurt<sup>w</sup> to thee, or doth owe anything, this to me be reckoning;
<sup>19</sup> I, Paul did write with my hand, I—I will repay; that I may not say that also thyself, besides, to me thou dost owe.
<sup>20</sup> Yes, brother, may I have profit of thee in the Lord; refresh my bowels in the Lord;

<sup>21</sup> having been confident in thy obedience<sup>x</sup> I did write to thee, having known that also above what I may say thou wilt do;
<sup>22</sup> and at the same time also prepare for me a lodging, for I hope that through your prayers I shall be granted to you.

<sup>23</sup> Salute<sup>y</sup> thee doth Epaphras,<sup>z</sup> (my fellow-captive in Christ Jesus,)
<sup>24</sup> Markus,<sup>aa</sup> Aristarchus,<sup>ab</sup> Demas,<sup>ac</sup> Lukas,<sup>ad</sup> my fellow-workmen!

<sup>25</sup> The grace<sup>ae</sup> of our Lord Jesus Christ _is_ with your spirit! Amen.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. PHILEMON lived at Colosse, and was a rich convert and fellow-labourer of Paul, who, when a prisoner in Rome, A.D. 61–2, wrote him this letter to induce him to receive back a run-away bondsman—Onesimus—whom Paul had met with and converted to the gospel. This letter was quoted or referred to by Tertullian (A.D. 192), Caius (A.D. 212), Origen (A.D. 230), Jerome (A.D. 392), Eusebius (A.D. 320), and perhaps Ignatius (A.D. 107).<br>THIS LETTER may be divided into six parts; v. 1–3 address and salutation; v. 4–7 thanksgiving for Philemon’s state; v. 8–16 entreaty for Onesimus; v. 17–19 personal reasons for this; v. 20–22 expression of confidence and a request; v. 23–25 closing salutations.
> - <sup>b</sup> 1. Lit., _bound one._
> - <sup>c</sup> 1. the “anointed saviour.”
> - <sup>d</sup> 1. “the brother,” in the faith.
> - <sup>e</sup> 1. “loving, befriending.”
> - <sup>f</sup> 1. “joint worker,” in building up the Colossians.
> - <sup>g</sup> 2. …Apphia (a female), and Archippus (i.e. chief-horseman, Col. 4. 17), …church (or assembly) at thy house; see Romans 16:15; 1 Corinthians 16:11
> - <sup>h</sup> 3. Lit., _what causes leaping for joy._
> - <sup>i</sup> 3. Lit., _what brings into unity._
> - <sup>j</sup> 3. Or, _and (Father of the) Lord Jesus Christ._
> - <sup>k</sup> 4. Lit., _leap much for joy_
> - <sup>l</sup> 4. Lit., _pouring-forth_
> - <sup>m</sup> 5. Lit., _“not of the earth.”_
> - <sup>n</sup> 7. Lit., _…exhortation…_
> - <sup>o</sup> 8. Lit., _…full-speech to arrange for thee what comes up._
> - <sup>p</sup> 9. Lit., _…call upon (thee)._
> - <sup>q</sup> 10. profitable.
> - <sup>r</sup> 13. Lit., _…he might be a deacon to me._
> - <sup>s</sup> 14. Lit., _apart from thy knowledge_
> - <sup>t</sup> 15. Lit., _was separated_
> - <sup>u</sup> 15. Lit., _…have him entirely;_ see Exodus 21:6
> - <sup>v</sup> 16. Lit., _no more as a bondsman._
> - <sup>w</sup> 18. Lit., _…used thee unjustly…_
> - <sup>x</sup> 21. Lit., _…submissive hearkening…_
> - <sup>y</sup> 23. Lit., _draw thee near_
> - <sup>z</sup> 23. Epaphroditus, upon Venus, i.q. lovely.
> - <sup>aa</sup> 24. “far, distant.”
> - <sup>ab</sup> 24. “best of chiefs.”
> - <sup>ac</sup> 24. a “plebeian,” of the “people.”
> - <sup>ad</sup> 24. “shining.”
> - <sup>ae</sup> 25. Lit., _what causes leaping for joy._
