## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name _was_ Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.
<sup>2</sup> And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters.
<sup>3</sup> His substance<sup>a</sup> also was seven thousand sheep, and three thousand camels, and five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she asses, and a very great household;<sup>b</sup> so that this man was the greatest of all the men of the east.<sup>c</sup>
<sup>4</sup> And his sons went and feasted _in their_ houses, every one his day; and sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them.
<sup>5</sup> And it was so, when the days of _their_ feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings _according_ to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually.<sup>d</sup>

### Job’s Character

<sup>6</sup> Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan<sup>e</sup> came also among them.<sup>f</sup>
<sup>7</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, Whence comest thou? Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it.
<sup>8</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered<sup>g</sup> my servant Job, that _there is_ none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?
<sup>9</sup> Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, Doth Job fear God for nought?
<sup>10</sup> Hast not thou made an hedge about him, and about his house, and about all that he hath on every side? thou hast blessed the work of his hands, and his substance<sup>h</sup> is increased in the land.
<sup>11</sup> But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face.<sup>i</sup>
<sup>12</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, all that he hath _is_ in thy power;<sup>j</sup> only upon himself put not forth thine hand. So Satan went forth from the presence of the LORD.

### His Afflictions

<sup>13</sup> And there was a day when his sons and his daughters _were_ eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house:
<sup>14</sup> and there came a messenger unto Job, and said, The oxen were plowing, and the asses feeding beside them:
<sup>15</sup> and the Sabeans fell _upon them_, and took them away; yea, they have slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.
<sup>16</sup> While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The fire of God<sup>k</sup> is fallen from heaven, and hath burned up the sheep, and the servants, and consumed them; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.
<sup>17</sup> While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The Chaldeans made out three bands, and fell<sup>l</sup> upon the camels, and have carried them away, yea, and slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.
<sup>18</sup> While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, Thy sons and thy daughters _were_ eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house:
<sup>19</sup> and, behold, there came a great wind from the<sup>m</sup> wilderness, and smote the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.

<sup>20</sup> Then Job arose, and rent his mantle,<sup>n</sup> and shaved his head, and fell down upon the ground, and worshipped,
<sup>21</sup> and said, Naked came I out of my mother’s womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.
<sup>22</sup> In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly.<sup>o</sup>

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> Again there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them to present himself before the LORD.
<sup>2</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, From whence comest thou? And Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it.
<sup>3</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that _there is_ none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil? and still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him<sup>a</sup> without cause.
<sup>4</sup> And Satan answered the LORD, and said, Skin for skin, yea, all that a man hath will he give for his life.
<sup>5</sup> But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse thee to thy face.
<sup>6</sup> And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, he _is_ in thine hand; but save<sup>b</sup> his life.

<sup>7</sup> So went Satan forth from the presence of the LORD, and smote Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot unto his crown.
<sup>8</sup> And he took him a potsherd to scrape himself withal; and he sat down among the ashes.
<sup>9</sup> Then said his wife unto him, Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die.
<sup>10</sup> But he said unto her, Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh. What? shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil? In all this did not Job sin with his lips.

### His Friends

<sup>11</sup> Now when Job’s three friends heard of all this evil that was come upon him, they came every one from his own place; Eliphaz the Temanite, and Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite: for they had made an appointment together to come to mourn with him and to comfort him.
<sup>12</sup> And when they lifted up their eyes afar off, and knew him not, they lifted up their voice, and wept; and they rent every one his mantle, and sprinkled dust upon their heads toward heaven.
<sup>13</sup> So they sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him: for they saw that _his_ grief was very great.

## Chapter 3

### The Debate


### Job’s Outburst

<sup>1</sup> After this opened Job his mouth, and cursed his day.
<sup>2</sup> And Job spake,<sup>a</sup> and said,<br>
<sup>3</sup> Let the day perish wherein I was born,<br>And the night _in which_ it was said,<br>There is a man child conceived.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Let that day be darkness;<br>Let not God regard it from above,<br>Neither let the light shine upon it.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Let darkness and the shadow of death stain it;<sup>b</sup><br>Let a cloud dwell upon it;<br>Let the blackness of the day terrify it.<sup>c</sup><br>
<sup>6</sup> _As for_ that night, let darkness seize upon it;<br>Let it not be joined unto the days<sup>d</sup> of the year,<br>Let it not come into the number of the months.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Lo, let that night be solitary,<br>Let no joyful voice come therein.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Let them curse it that curse the day,<br>Who are ready to raise up their mourning.<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>9</sup> Let the stars of the twilight thereof be dark;<br>Let it look for light, but _have_ none;<br>Neither let it see the dawning of the day:<sup>f</sup><br>
<sup>10</sup> Because it shut not up the doors of my _mother’s_ womb,<br>Nor hid sorrow from mine eyes.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Why died I not from the womb?<br>_Why_ did I _not_ give up the ghost<br>When I came out of the belly?<br>
<sup>12</sup> Why did the knees prevent me?<br>Or why the breasts that I should suck?<br>
<sup>13</sup> For now should I have lain still and been quiet,<br>I should have slept: then had I been at rest,<br>
<sup>14</sup> With kings and counsellors of the earth,<br>Which built desolate places for themselves;<br>
<sup>15</sup> Or with princes that had gold,<br>Who filled their houses with silver:<br>
<sup>16</sup> Or as an hidden untimely birth I had not been;<br>As infants _which_ never saw light.<br>
<sup>17</sup> There the wicked cease _from_ troubling;<br>And there the weary be at rest.<sup>g</sup><br>
<sup>18</sup> _There_ the prisoners rest together;<br>They hear not the voice of the oppressor.<br>
<sup>19</sup> The small and great are there;<br>And the servant _is_ free from his master.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Wherefore is light given to him that is in misery,<br>And life unto the bitter _in_ soul;<br>
<sup>21</sup> Which long<sup>h</sup> for death, but it _cometh_ not;<br>And dig for it more than for hid treasures;<br>
<sup>22</sup> Which rejoice exceedingly,<br>_And_ are glad, when they can find the grave?<br>
<sup>23</sup> _Why is light given_ to a man whose way is hid,<br>And whom God hath hedged in?<br>
<sup>24</sup> For my sighing cometh before I eat,<sup>i</sup><br>And my roarings are poured out like the waters.<br>
<sup>25</sup> For the thing which I greatly feared is come upon me,<sup>j</sup><br>And that which I was afraid of is come unto me.<br>
<sup>26</sup> I was not in safety, neither had I rest, neither was I quiet;<br>Yet trouble came.<br>

## Chapter 4

### Eliphaz Speaks

<sup>1</sup> Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> _If_ we assay to commune<sup>a</sup> with thee, wilt thou be grieved?<br>But who can withhold himself from speaking?<br>
<sup>3</sup> Behold, thou hast instructed many,<br>And thou hast strengthened the weak hands.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Thy words have upholden him that was falling,<br>And thou hast strengthened the feeble knees.<sup>b</sup><br>
<sup>5</sup> But now it is come upon thee, and thou faintest;<br>It toucheth thee, and thou art troubled.<br>
<sup>6</sup> _Is_ not _this_ thy fear, thy confidence,<br>Thy hope, and the uprightness of thy ways?<br>
<sup>7</sup> Remember, I pray thee, who _ever_ perished, being innocent?<br>Or where were the righteous cut off?<br>
<sup>8</sup> Even as I have seen, they that plow iniquity,<br>And sow wickedness, reap the same.<br>
<sup>9</sup> By the blast of God they perish,<br>And by the breath of his nostrils are they consumed.<br>
<sup>10</sup> The roaring of the lion, and the voice of the fierce lion,<br>And the teeth of the young lions, are broken.<br>
<sup>11</sup> The old lion perisheth for lack of prey,<br>And the stout lion’s whelps are scattered abroad.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Now a thing was secretly<sup>c</sup> brought to me,<br>And mine ear received a little thereof.<br>
<sup>13</sup> In thoughts from the visions of the night,<br>When deep sleep falleth on men,<br>
<sup>14</sup> Fear came upon me,<sup>d</sup> and trembling,<br>Which made all my bones<sup>e</sup> to shake.<br>
<sup>15</sup> Then a spirit passed before my face;<br>The hair of my flesh stood up:<br>
<sup>16</sup> It stood still,<br>But I could not discern the form thereof:<br>An image _was_ before mine eyes,<br>_There was_ silence,<sup>f</sup> and I heard a voice, _saying_,<br>
<sup>17</sup> Shall mortal man be more just than God?<br>Shall a man be more pure than his maker?<br>
<sup>18</sup> Behold, he put no trust in his servants;<br>And his angels he charged<sup>g</sup> with folly:<br>
<sup>19</sup> How much less _in_ them that dwell in houses of clay,<br>Whose foundation _is_ in the dust,<br>_Which_ are crushed before the moth?<br>
<sup>20</sup> They are destroyed<sup>h</sup> from morning to evening:<br>They perish for ever without any regarding _it_.<br>
<sup>21</sup> Doth not their excellency _which is_ in them go away?<br>They die, even without wisdom.<br>

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Call now, if there be any that will answer thee;<br>And to which of the saints wilt thou turn?<sup>a</sup><br>
<sup>2</sup> For wrath killeth the foolish man,<br>And envy<sup>b</sup> slayeth the silly one.<br>
<sup>3</sup> I have seen the foolish taking root:<br>But suddenly I cursed his habitation.<br>
<sup>4</sup> His children are far from safety,<br>And they are crushed in the gate,<br>Neither _is there_ any to deliver _them_.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Whose harvest the hungry eateth up,<br>And taketh it even out of the thorns,<br>And the robber swalloweth up their substance.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Although affliction<sup>c</sup> cometh not forth of the dust,<br>Neither doth trouble spring out of the ground;<br>
<sup>7</sup> Yet man is born unto trouble,<sup>d</sup><br>As the sparks fly upward.<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>8</sup> I would seek unto God,<br>And unto God would I commit my cause:<br>

<sup>10</sup> Who giveth rain upon the earth,<br>And sendeth waters upon the fields:<sup>h</sup><br>
<sup>11</sup> To set up on high those that be low;<br>That those which mourn may be exalted to safety.<br>
<sup>12</sup> He disappointeth the devices of the crafty,<br>So that their hands cannot perform _their_ enterprise.<sup>i</sup><br>
<sup>13</sup> He taketh the wise in their own craftiness:<br>And the counsel of the froward is carried headlong.<br>

<sup>20</sup> In famine he shall redeem thee from death:<br>And in war from the power<sup>k</sup> of the sword.<br>
<sup>21</sup> Thou shalt be hid from the scourge of the tongue:<sup>l</sup><br>Neither shalt thou be afraid of destruction when it cometh.<br>
<sup>22</sup> At destruction and famine thou shalt laugh:<br>Neither shalt thou be afraid of the beasts of the earth.<br>
<sup>23</sup> For thou shalt be in league with the stones of the field:<br>And the beasts of the field shall be at peace with thee.<br>
<sup>24</sup> And thou shalt know that thy tabernacle _shall be_ in peace;<sup>m</sup><br>And thou shalt visit thy habitation, and shalt not sin.<sup>n</sup><br>
<sup>25</sup> Thou shalt know also that thy seed _shall be_ great,<sup>o</sup><br>And thine offspring as the grass of the earth.<br>
<sup>26</sup> Thou shalt come to _thy_ grave in a full age,<br>Like as a shock of corn cometh in<sup>p</sup> in his season.<br>
<sup>27</sup> Lo this, we have searched it, so it _is_;<br>Hear it, and know thou _it_ for thy good.<sup>q</sup><br>

## Chapter 6

### Job’s Answer

<sup>1</sup> But Job answered and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> Oh that my grief were throughly weighed,<br>And my calamity laid<sup>a</sup> in the balances together!<br>
<sup>3</sup> For now it would be heavier than the sand of the sea:<br>Therefore my words are swallowed up.<sup>b</sup><br>
<sup>4</sup> For the arrows of the Almighty _are_ within me,<br>The poison whereof drinketh up my spirit:<br>The terrors of God do set themselves in array against me.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Doth the wild ass bray when he hath grass?<sup>c</sup><br>Or loweth the ox over his fodder?<br>
<sup>6</sup> Can that which is unsavoury be eaten without salt?<br>Or is there _any_ taste in the white of an egg?<br>
<sup>7</sup> The things _that_ my soul refused to touch<br>_Are_ as my sorrowful meat.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Oh that I might have my request;<br>And that God would grant _me_ the thing that I long for!<sup>d</sup><br>
<sup>9</sup> Even that it would please God to destroy me;<br>That he would let loose his hand, and cut me off!<br>
<sup>10</sup> Then should I yet have comfort;<br>Yea, I would harden myself in sorrow: let him not spare;<br>For I have not concealed the words of the Holy One.<br>
<sup>11</sup> What _is_ my strength, that I should hope?<br>And what _is_ mine end, that I should prolong my life?<br>
<sup>12</sup> _Is_ my strength the strength of stones?<br>Or _is_ my flesh of brass?<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>13</sup> _Is_ not my help in me?<br>And is wisdom driven quite from me?<br>
<sup>14</sup> To him that is afflicted<sup>f</sup> pity _should be shewed_ from his friend;<br>But he forsaketh the fear of the Almighty.<br>
<sup>15</sup> My brethren have dealt deceitfully as a brook,<br>_And_ as the stream of brooks they pass away;<br>
<sup>16</sup> Which are blackish by reason of the ice,<br>_And_ wherein the snow is hid:<br>
<sup>17</sup> What time they wax warm, they vanish:<sup>g</sup><br>When it is hot,<sup>h</sup> they are consumed<sup>i</sup> out of their place.<br>
<sup>18</sup> The paths of their way are turned aside;<br>They go to nothing, and perish.<br>
<sup>19</sup> The troops of Tema looked,<br>The companies of Sheba waited for them.<br>
<sup>20</sup> They were confounded because they had hoped;<br>They came thither, and were ashamed.<br>
<sup>21</sup> For now ye are<sup>j</sup> nothing;<sup>k</sup><br>Ye see _my_ casting down, and are afraid.<br>
<sup>22</sup> Did I say, Bring unto me?<br>Or, Give a reward for me of your substance?<br>
<sup>23</sup> Or, Deliver me from the enemy’s hand?<br>Or, Redeem me from the hand of the mighty?<br>
<sup>24</sup> Teach me, and I will hold my tongue:<br>And cause me to understand wherein I have erred.<br>
<sup>25</sup> How forcible are right words!<br>But what doth your arguing reprove?<br>
<sup>26</sup> Do ye imagine to reprove words,<br>And the speeches of one that is desperate, _which are_ as wind?<br>
<sup>27</sup> Yea, ye overwhelm<sup>l</sup> the fatherless,<br>And ye dig _a pit_ for your friend.<br>
<sup>28</sup> Now therefore be content, look upon me;<br>For _it is_ evident unto you<sup>m</sup> if I lie.<br>
<sup>29</sup> Return, I pray you, let it not be iniquity;<br>Yea, return again, my righteousness _is_ in it.<sup>n</sup><br>
<sup>30</sup> Is there iniquity in my tongue?<br>Cannot my taste<sup>o</sup> discern perverse things?<br>

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> _Is there_ not an appointed time<sup>a</sup> to man upon earth?<br>_Are not_ his days also like the days of an hireling?<br>
<sup>2</sup> As a servant earnestly desireth<sup>b</sup> the shadow,<br>And as an hireling looketh for _the reward of_ his work:<br>
<sup>3</sup> So am I made to possess months of vanity,<br>And wearisome nights are appointed to me.<br>
<sup>4</sup> When I lie down, I say,<br>When shall I arise, and the night be gone?<sup>c</sup><br>And I am full of tossings to and fro unto the dawning of the day.<br>
<sup>5</sup> My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust;<br>My skin is broken, and become loathsome.<br>
<sup>6</sup> My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle,<br>And are spent without hope.<br>
<sup>7</sup> O remember that my life _is_ wind:<br>Mine eye shall no more<sup>d</sup> see good.<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>8</sup> The eye of him that hath seen me shall see me no _more:_<br>Thine eyes _are_ upon me, and I _am_ not.<sup>f</sup><br>
<sup>9</sup> _As_ the cloud is consumed and vanisheth away:<br>So he that goeth down to the grave shall come up no _more_.<br>
<sup>10</sup> He shall return no more to his house,<br>Neither shall his place know him any more.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Therefore I will not refrain my mouth;<br>I will speak in the anguish of my spirit;<br>I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.<br>
<sup>12</sup> _Am_ I a sea, or a whale,<br>That thou settest a watch over me?<br>
<sup>13</sup> When I say, My bed shall comfort me,<br>My couch shall ease my complaint;<br>
<sup>14</sup> Then thou scarest me with dreams,<br>And terrifiest me through visions:<br>
<sup>15</sup> So that my soul chooseth strangling,<br>_And_ death rather than my life.<br>
<sup>16</sup> I loathe _it_; I would not live alway:<br>Let me alone; for my days _are_ vanity.<br>
<sup>17</sup> What _is_ man, that thou shouldest magnify him?<br>And that thou shouldest set thine heart upon him?<br>
<sup>18</sup> And _that_ thou shouldest visit him every morning,<br>_And_ try him every moment?<br>

<sup>1</sup> Then answered Eliphaz the Temanite, and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> Should a wise man utter vain knowledge,<sup>a</sup><br>And fill his belly with the east wind?<br>
<sup>3</sup> Should he reason with unprofitable talk?<br>Or with speeches wherewith he can do no good?<br>
<sup>4</sup> Yea, thou castest off fear,<br>And restrainest prayer<sup>b</sup> before God.<br>
<sup>5</sup> For thy mouth uttereth<sup>c</sup> thine iniquity,<br>And thou choosest the tongue of the crafty.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Thine own mouth condemneth thee, and not I:<br>Yea, thine own lips testify against thee.<br>
<sup>7</sup> _Art_ thou the first man _that_ was born?<br>Or wast thou made before the hills?<br>
<sup>8</sup> Hast thou heard the secret of God?<br>And dost thou restrain wisdom to thyself?<br>
<sup>9</sup> What knowest thou, that we know not?<br>_What_ understandest thou, which _is_ not in us?<br>
<sup>10</sup> With us _are_ both the grayheaded and very aged men,<br>Much elder than thy father.<br>
<sup>11</sup> _Are_ the consolations of God small with thee?<br>Is there any secret thing with thee?<br>
<sup>12</sup> Why doth thine heart carry thee away?<br>And what do thy eyes wink at,<br>
<sup>13</sup> That thou turnest thy spirit against God,<br>And lettest _such_ words go out of thy mouth?<br>
<sup>14</sup> What _is_ man, that he should be clean?<br>And _he which is_ born of a woman, that he should be righteous?<br>
<sup>15</sup> Behold, he putteth no trust in his saints;<br>Yea, the heavens are not clean in his sight.<br>
<sup>16</sup> How much more abominable and filthy _is_ man,<br>Which drinketh iniquity like water?<br>
<sup>17</sup> I will shew thee, hear me;<br>And that _which_ I have seen I will declare;<br>
<sup>18</sup> Which wise men have told<br>From their fathers, and have not hid _it:_<br>
<sup>19</sup> Unto whom alone the earth was given,<br>And no stranger passed among them.<br>
<sup>20</sup> The wicked man travaileth with pain all _his_ days,<br>And the number of years is hidden to the oppressor.<br>
<sup>21</sup> A dreadful sound<sup>d</sup> _is_ in his ears:<br>In prosperity the destroyer shall come upon him.<br>
<sup>22</sup> He believeth not that he shall return out of darkness,<br>And he is waited for of the sword.<br>
<sup>23</sup> He wandereth abroad for bread, _saying_, Where _is it?_<br>He knoweth that the day of darkness is ready at his hand.<br>
<sup>24</sup> Trouble and anguish shall make him afraid;<br>They shall prevail against him, as a king ready to the battle.<br>
<sup>25</sup> For he stretcheth out his hand against God,<br>And strengtheneth himself against the Almighty.<br>
<sup>26</sup> He runneth upon him, _even_ on _his_ neck,<br>Upon the thick bosses of his bucklers:<br>
<sup>27</sup> Because he covereth his face with his fatness,<br>And maketh collops of fat on _his_ flanks.<br>
<sup>28</sup> And he dwelleth in desolate cities,<br>_And_ in houses which no man inhabiteth,<br>Which are ready to become heaps.<br>
<sup>29</sup> He shall not be rich, neither shall his substance continue,<br>Neither shall he prolong the perfection thereof upon the earth.<br>
<sup>30</sup> He shall not depart out of darkness;<br>The flame shall dry up his branches,<br>And by the breath of his mouth shall he go away.<br>
<sup>31</sup> Let not him that is deceived trust in vanity:<br>For vanity shall be his recompence.<br>
<sup>32</sup> It shall be accomplished<sup>e</sup> before his time,<br>And his branch shall not be green.<br>
<sup>33</sup> He shall shake off his unripe grape as the vine,<br>And shall cast off his flower as the olive.<br>
<sup>34</sup> For the congregation of hypocrites _shall be_ desolate,<br>And fire shall consume the tabernacles of bribery.<br>
<sup>35</sup> They conceive mischief, and bring forth vanity,<sup>f</sup><br>And their belly prepareth deceit.<br>

## Chapter 16

### Job’s Reply

<sup>1</sup> Then Job answered and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> I have heard many such things:<br>Miserable<sup>a</sup> comforters _are_ ye all.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Shall vain words<sup>b</sup> have an end?<br>Or what emboldeneth thee that thou answerest?<br>
<sup>4</sup> I also could speak as ye _do:_<br>If your soul were in my soul’s stead,<br>I could heap up words against you,<br>And shake mine head at you.<br>
<sup>5</sup> _But_ I would strengthen you with my mouth,<br>And the moving of my lips should asswage _your grief_.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Though I speak, my grief is not asswaged:<br>And _though_ I forbear, what am I eased?<sup>c</sup><br>
<sup>7</sup> But now he hath made me weary:<br>Thou hast made desolate all my company.<br>
<sup>8</sup> And thou hast filled me with wrinkles, _which_ is a witness _against me:_<br>And my leanness rising up in me beareth witness to my face.<br>
<sup>9</sup> He teareth _me_ in his wrath, who hateth me:<br>He gnasheth upon me with his teeth;<br>Mine enemy sharpeneth his eyes upon me.<br>
<sup>10</sup> They have gaped upon me with their mouth;<br>They have smitten me upon the cheek reproachfully;<br>They have gathered themselves together against me.<br>
<sup>11</sup> God hath delivered me<sup>d</sup> to the ungodly,<br>And turned me over into the hands of the wicked.<br>
<sup>12</sup> I was at ease, but he hath broken me asunder:<br>He hath also taken _me_ by my neck, and shaken me to pieces,<br>And set me up for his mark.<br>
<sup>13</sup> His archers compass me round about,<br>He cleaveth my reins asunder, and doth not spare;<br>He poureth out my gall upon the ground.<br>
<sup>14</sup> He breaketh me with breach upon breach,<br>He runneth upon me like a giant.<br>
<sup>15</sup> I have sewed sackcloth upon my skin,<br>And defiled my horn in the dust.<br>
<sup>16</sup> My face is foul with weeping,<br>And on my eyelids _is_ the shadow of death;<br>
<sup>17</sup> Not for _any_ injustice in mine hands:<br>Also my prayer _is_ pure.<br>
<sup>18</sup> O earth, cover not thou my blood,<br>And let my cry have no place.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Also now, behold, my witness _is_ in heaven,<br>And my record _is_ on high.<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>20</sup> My friends scorn me:<sup>f</sup><br>_but_ mine eye poureth out _tears_ unto God.<br>
<sup>21</sup> O that one might plead for a man with God,<br>As a man _pleadeth_ for his neighbour!<br>
<sup>22</sup> When a few years<sup>g</sup> are come,<br>Then I shall go the way _whence_ I shall not return.<br>

<sup>6</sup> He hath made me also a byword of the people;<br>And aforetime<sup>c</sup> I was as a tabret.<br>

<sup>10</sup> But as for you all, do ye return, and come now:<br>For I cannot find _one_ wise _man_ among you.<br>
<sup>11</sup> My days are past, my purposes are broken off,<br>_Even_ the thoughts<sup>f</sup> of my heart.<br>
<sup>12</sup> They change the night into day:<br>The light _is_ short<sup>g</sup> because of darkness.<br>
<sup>13</sup> If I wait, the grave _is_ mine house:<br>I have made my bed in the darkness.<br>

<sup>15</sup> And where _is_ now my hope?<br>As for my hope, who shall see it?<br>
<sup>16</sup> They shall go down to the bars of the pit,<br>When _our_ rest together _is_ in the dust.<br>

<sup>7</sup> The steps of his strength shall be straitened,<br>And his own counsel shall cast him down.<br>
<sup>8</sup> For he is cast into a net by his own feet,<br>And he walketh upon a snare.<br>
<sup>9</sup> The gin shall take _him_ by the heel,<br>_And_ the robber shall prevail against him.<br>
<sup>10</sup> The snare _is_ laid<sup>c</sup> for him in the ground,<br>And a trap for him in the way.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Terrors shall make him afraid on every side,<br>And shall drive him<sup>d</sup> to his feet.<br>
<sup>12</sup> His strength shall be hungerbitten,<br>And destruction _shall be_ ready at his side.<br>
<sup>13</sup> It shall devour the strength<sup>e</sup> of his skin:<br>_Even_ the firstborn of death shall devour his strength.<br>
<sup>14</sup> His confidence shall be rooted out of his tabernacle,<br>And it shall bring him to the king of terrors.<br>

## Chapter 20

### Zophar’s Speech

<sup>1</sup> Then answered Zophar the Naamathite, and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> Therefore do my thoughts cause me to answer,<br>And for _this_ I make haste.<sup>a</sup><br>
<sup>3</sup> I have heard the check of my reproach,<br>And the spirit of my understanding causeth me to answer.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Knowest thou _not_ this of old,<br>Since man was placed upon earth,<br>
<sup>5</sup> That the triumphing of the wicked _is_ short,<sup>b</sup><br>And the joy of the hypocrite _but_ for a moment?<br>
<sup>6</sup> Though his excellency mount up to the heavens,<br>And his head reach unto the clouds;<sup>c</sup><br>
<sup>7</sup> _Yet_ he shall perish for ever like his own dung:<br>They which have seen him shall say, Where _is_ he?<br>
<sup>8</sup> He shall fly away as a dream, and shall not be found:<br>Yea, he shall be chased away as a vision of the night.<br>
<sup>9</sup> The eye also _which_ saw him shall _see him_ no more;<br>Neither shall his place any more behold him.<br>
<sup>10</sup> His children shall seek to please the poor,<sup>d</sup><br>And his hands shall restore their goods.<br>
<sup>11</sup> His bones are full _of the sin_ of his youth,<br>Which shall lie down with him in the dust.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Though wickedness be sweet in his mouth,<br>_Though_ he hide it under his tongue;<br>
<sup>13</sup> _Though_ he spare it, and forsake it not;<br>But keep it still within his mouth:<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>14</sup> _Yet_ his meat in his bowels is turned,<br>_It is_ the gall of asps within him.<br>
<sup>15</sup> He hath swallowed down riches, and he shall vomit them up again:<br>God shall cast them out of his belly.<br>
<sup>16</sup> He shall suck the poison of asps:<br>The viper’s tongue shall slay him.<br>
<sup>17</sup> He shall not see the rivers,<br>The floods, the brooks<sup>f</sup> of honey and butter.<br>
<sup>18</sup> That which he laboured for shall he restore,<br>And shall not swallow _it_ down:<br>According to _his_ substance _shall_ the restitution _be_,<sup>g</sup><br>and he shall not rejoice _therein_.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Because he hath oppressed<sup>h</sup> _and_ hath forsaken the poor;<br>_Because_ he hath violently taken away an house which he builded not;<br>
<sup>20</sup> Surely he shall not feel<sup>i</sup> quietness in his belly,<br>He shall not save of that which he desired.<br>
<sup>21</sup> There shall none of his meat be left;<sup>j</sup><br>Therefore shall no man look for his goods.<br>
<sup>22</sup> In the fulness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits:<br>Every hand of the wicked<sup>k</sup> shall come upon him.<br>
<sup>23</sup> _When_ he is about to fill his belly,<br>_God_ shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him,<br>And shall rain _it_ upon him while he is eating.<br>
<sup>24</sup> He shall flee from the iron weapon,<br>_And_ the bow of steel shall strike him through.<br>
<sup>25</sup> It is drawn, and cometh out of the body;<br>Yea, the glittering sword cometh out of his gall:<br>Terrors _are_ upon him.<br>
<sup>26</sup> All darkness _shall be_ hid in his secret places:<br>A fire not blown shall consume him;<br>It shall go ill with him that is left in his tabernacle.<br>
<sup>27</sup> The heaven shall reveal his iniquity;<br>And the earth shall rise up against him.<br>
<sup>28</sup> The increase of his house shall depart,<br>_And his goods_ shall flow away in the day of his wrath.<br>
<sup>29</sup> This _is_ the portion of a wicked man from God,<br>And the heritage appointed unto him by God.<sup>l</sup><br>

## Chapter 21

### Job’s Reply

<sup>1</sup> But Job answered and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> Hear diligently my speech,<br>And let this be your consolations.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Suffer me that I may speak;<br>And after that I have spoken, mock on.<br>
<sup>4</sup> As for me, _is_ my complaint to man?<br>And if _it were so_, why should not my spirit be troubled?<sup>a</sup><br>
<sup>5</sup> Mark me,<sup>b</sup> and be astonished,<br>And lay _your_ hand upon _your_ mouth.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Even when I remember I am afraid,<br>And trembling taketh hold on my flesh.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Wherefore do the wicked live,<br>Become old, yea, are mighty in power?<br>
<sup>8</sup> Their seed is established in their sight with them,<br>And their offspring before their eyes.<br>
<sup>9</sup> Their houses _are_ safe from fear,<sup>c</sup><br>Neither _is_ the rod of God upon them.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Their bull gendereth, and faileth not;<br>Their cow calveth, and casteth not her calf.<br>
<sup>11</sup> They send forth their little ones like a flock,<br>And their children dance.<br>
<sup>12</sup> They take the timbrel and harp,<br>And rejoice at the sound of the organ.<br>
<sup>13</sup> They spend their days in wealth,<sup>d</sup><br>And in a moment go down to the grave.<br>
<sup>14</sup> Therefore they say unto God, Depart from us;<br>For we desire not the knowledge of thy ways.<br>
<sup>15</sup> What _is_ the Almighty, that we should serve him?<br>And what profit should we have, if we pray unto him?<br>
<sup>16</sup> Lo, their good _is_ not in their hand:<br>The counsel of the wicked is far from me.<br>
<sup>17</sup> How oft is the candle<sup>e</sup> of the wicked put out!<br>And _how oft_ cometh their destruction upon them!<br>_God_ distributeth sorrows in his anger.<br>
<sup>18</sup> They are as stubble before the wind,<br>And as chaff that the storm carrieth away.<sup>f</sup><br>
<sup>19</sup> God layeth up his iniquity<sup>g</sup> for his children:<br>He rewardeth him, and he shall know _it_.<br>
<sup>20</sup> His eyes shall see his destruction,<br>And he shall drink of the wrath of the Almighty.<br>
<sup>21</sup> For what pleasure _hath_ he in his house after him,<br>When the number of his months is cut off in the midst?<br>
<sup>22</sup> Shall _any_ teach God knowledge?<br>Seeing he judgeth those that are high.<br>
<sup>23</sup> One dieth in his full strength,<sup>h</sup><br>Being wholly at ease and quiet.<br>
<sup>24</sup> His breasts<sup>i</sup> are full of milk,<br>And his bones are moistened with marrow.<br>
<sup>25</sup> And another dieth in the bitterness of his soul,<br>And never eateth with pleasure.<br>
<sup>26</sup> They shall lie down alike in the dust,<br>And the worms shall cover them.<br>
<sup>27</sup> Behold, I know your thoughts,<br>And the devices _which_ ye wrongfully imagine against me.<br>
<sup>28</sup> For ye say, Where _is_ the house of the prince?<br>And where _are_ the dwelling places<sup>j</sup> of the wicked?<br>

<sup>19</sup> Out of his mouth go burning lamps,<br>_And_ sparks of fire leap out.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Out of his nostrils goeth smoke,<br>As _out_ of a seething pot or caldron.<br>
<sup>21</sup> His breath kindleth coals,<br>And a flame goeth out of his mouth.<br>
<sup>22</sup> In his neck remaineth strength,<br>And sorrow is turned into joy<sup>e</sup> before him.<br>
<sup>23</sup> The flakes<sup>f</sup> of his flesh are joined together:<br>They are firm in themselves; they cannot be moved.<br>
<sup>24</sup> His heart is as firm as a stone;<br>Yea, as hard as a piece of the nether _millstone_.<br>
<sup>25</sup> When he raiseth up himself, the mighty are afraid:<br>By reason of breakings they purify themselves.<br>
<sup>26</sup> The sword of him that layeth at him cannot hold:<br>The spear, the dart, nor the habergeon.<sup>g</sup><br>
<sup>27</sup> He esteemeth iron as straw,<br>_And_ brass as rotten wood.<br>
<sup>28</sup> The arrow cannot make him flee:<br>Slingstones are turned with him into stubble.<br>
<sup>29</sup> Darts are counted as stubble:<br>He laugheth at the shaking of a spear.<br>
<sup>30</sup> Sharp stones<sup>h</sup> _are_ under him:<br>He spreadeth sharp pointed things upon the mire.<br>
<sup>31</sup> He maketh the deep to boil like a pot:<br>He maketh the sea like a pot of ointment.<br>
<sup>32</sup> He maketh a path to shine after him;<br>_One_ would think the deep _to be_ hoary.<br>
<sup>33</sup> Upon earth there is not his like,<br>Who is made without fear.<sup>i</sup><br>
<sup>34</sup> He beholdeth all high _things:_<br>He _is_ a king over all the children of pride.<br>

## Chapter 42

### Job’s Reply

<sup>1</sup> Then Job answered the LORD, and said,<br>
<sup>2</sup> I know that thou canst do every _thing_,<br>And _that_ no thought can be withholden<sup>a</sup> from thee.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Who _is_ he that hideth counsel without knowledge?<br>Therefore have I uttered that I understood not;<br>Things too wonderful for me, which I knew not.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Hear, I beseech thee, and I will speak:<br>I will demand of thee, and declare thou unto me.<br>
<sup>5</sup> I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear:<br>But now mine eye seeth thee.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Wherefore I abhor _myself_, and repent<br>In dust and ashes.<br>

### The Epilogue

<sup>7</sup> And it was _so_, that after the LORD had spoken these words unto Job, the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite, My wrath is kindled against thee, and against thy two friends: for ye have not spoken of me _the thing that is_ right, as my servant Job _hath_.
<sup>8</sup> Therefore take unto you now seven bullocks and seven rams, and go to my servant Job, and offer up for yourselves a burnt offering; and my servant Job shall pray for you: for him<sup>b</sup> will I accept: lest I deal with you _after your_ folly, in that ye have not spoken of me _the thing which is_ right, like my servant Job.
<sup>9</sup> So Eliphaz the Temanite and Bildad the Shuhite _and_ Zophar the Naamathite went, and did according as the LORD commanded them: the LORD also accepted Job.<sup>c</sup>
<sup>10</sup> And the LORD turned the captivity of Job, when he prayed for his friends: also the LORD gave Job twice as much<sup>d</sup> as he had before.
<sup>11</sup> Then came there unto him all his brethren, and all his sisters, and all they that had been of his acquaintance before, and did eat bread with him in his house: and they bemoaned him, and comforted him over all the evil that the LORD had brought upon him: every man also gave him a piece of money, and every one an earring of gold.
<sup>12</sup> So the LORD blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning: for he had fourteen thousand sheep, and six thousand camels, and a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses.
<sup>13</sup> He had also seven sons and three daughters.
<sup>14</sup> And he called the name of the first, Jemima; and the name of the second, Kezia; and the name of the third, Keren-happuch.
<sup>15</sup> And in all the land were no women found _so_ fair as the daughters of Job: and their father gave them inheritance among their brethren.
<sup>16</sup> After this lived Job an hundred and forty years, and saw his sons, and his sons’ sons, _even_ four generations.
<sup>17</sup> So Job died, _being_ old and full of days.
