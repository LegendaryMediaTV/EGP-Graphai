## Chapter 1

### The Bride Confesses Her Love<sup>a</sup>

<sup>1</sup> This is Solomon’s Song of Songs.<sup>b</sup>

### The Bride

<sup>2</sup> Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth!<br>For your love is more delightful than wine.<br>
<sup>3</sup> The fragrance of your perfume is pleasing;<br>your name is like perfume poured out.<br>No wonder the maidens adore you.

<sup>4</sup> Take me away with you—let us hurry!<br>May the king bring me to his chambers.
### The Friends


We will rejoice and delight in you;<br>we will praise your love more than wine.
### The Bride


It is only right that they adore you.

<sup>5</sup> I am dark, yet lovely, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>like the tents of Kedar, like the curtains of Solomon.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Do not stare because I am dark,<br>for the sun has gazed upon me.<br>My mother’s sons were angry with me;<br>they made me a keeper of the vineyards,<br>but my own vineyard I have neglected.

<sup>7</sup> Tell me, O one I love,<br>where do you pasture your sheep?<br>Where do you rest them at midday?<br>Why should I be like a veiled woman<br>beside the flocks of your companions?

### The Friends

<sup>8</sup> If you do not know, O fairest of women,<br>follow the tracks of the flock,<br>and graze your young goats<br>near the tents of the shepherds.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>9</sup> I compare you, my darling,<br>to a mare among Pharaoh’s chariots.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Your cheeks are beautiful with ornaments,<br>your neck with strings of jewels.

### The Friends

<sup>11</sup> We will make you ornaments of gold,<br>studded with beads of silver.

### The Bride

<sup>12</sup> While the king was at his table,<br>my perfume spread its fragrance.<br>
<sup>13</sup> My beloved is to me a sachet of myrrh<br>resting between my breasts.<br>
<sup>14</sup> My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms<br>in the vineyards of En-gedi.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>15</sup> How beautiful you are, my darling!<br>Oh, how very beautiful!<br>Your eyes are like doves.

### The Bride

<sup>16</sup> How handsome you are, my beloved!<br>Oh, how delightful!<br>The soft grass is our bed.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>17</sup> The beams of our house are cedars;<br>our rafters are fragrant firs.

> - <sup>a</sup> Heading. Ephesians 5:22–33; 1 Peter 3:1–7
> - <sup>b</sup> 1. Most translators add subheadings for speaker identifications such as _The Bride_, _The Groom_, and _The Friends_ based on the gender and number of the Hebrew words.

## Chapter 2

### The Bride’s Admiration


### The Bride

<sup>1</sup> I am a rose of Sharon,<sup>a</sup><br>a lily of the valley.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>2</sup> Like a lily among the thorns<br>is my darling among the maidens.

### The Bride

<sup>3</sup> Like an apple tree among the trees of the forest<br>is my beloved among the young men.<br>I delight to sit in his shade,<br>and his fruit is sweet to my taste.<br>
<sup>4</sup> He has brought me to the house of wine,<sup>b</sup><br>and his banner over me is love.

<sup>5</sup> Sustain me with raisins;<br>refresh me with apples,<br>for I am faint with love.

<sup>6</sup> His left hand is under my head,<br>and his right arm embraces me.<br>
<sup>7</sup> O daughters of Jerusalem, I adjure you<br>by the gazelles and does of the field:<br>Do not arouse or awaken love<br>until the time is right.

<sup>8</sup> Listen! My beloved approaches.<br>Look! Here he comes,<br>leaping across the mountains,<br>bounding over the hills.<br>
<sup>9</sup> My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag.<br>Look, he stands behind our wall,<br>gazing through the windows,<br>peering through the lattice.

<sup>10</sup> My beloved calls to me,<br>“Arise, my darling.<br>Come away with me, my beautiful one.<br>
<sup>11</sup> For now the winter is past;<br>the rain is over and gone.<br>
<sup>12</sup> The flowers have appeared in the countryside;<br>the season of singing<sup>c</sup> has come,<br>and the cooing of turtledoves<br>is heard in our land.<br>
<sup>13</sup> The fig tree ripens its figs;<br>the blossoming vines spread their fragrance.<br>Arise, come away, my darling;<br>come away with me, my beautiful one.”

### The Bridegroom

<sup>14</sup> O my dove in the clefts of the rock,<br>in the crevices of the cliff,<br>let me see your face,<br>let me hear your voice;<br>for your voice is sweet,<br>and your countenance is lovely.

### The Friends

<sup>15</sup> Catch for us the foxes—<br>the little foxes that ruin the vineyards—<br>for our vineyards are in bloom.

### The Bride

<sup>16</sup> My beloved is mine and I am his;<br>he pastures his flock among the lilies.<br>
<sup>17</sup> Before the day breaks and shadows flee,<br>turn, my beloved,<br>and be like a gazelle<br>or a young stag on the mountains of Bether.<sup>d</sup>

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. Sharon Plain is a region in the coastal plain of Israel
> - <sup>b</sup> 4. That is, the banquet hall
> - <sup>c</sup> 12. Or _pruning_
> - <sup>d</sup> 17. Or _the rugged mountains_

## Chapter 3

### The Bride’s Dream

<sup>1</sup> On my bed at night<br>I sought the one I love;<br>I sought him,<br>but did not find him.<br>
<sup>2</sup> I will arise now and go about the city,<br>through the streets and squares.<br>I will seek the one I love.<br>So I sought him but did not find him.

<sup>3</sup> I encountered the watchmen on their rounds of the city:<br>“Have you seen the one I love?”<br>
<sup>4</sup> I had just passed them when I found the one I love.<br>I held him and would not let go<br>until I had brought him to my mother’s house,<br>to the chamber of the one who conceived me.

<sup>5</sup> O daughters of Jerusalem, I adjure you<br>by the gazelles and does of the field:<br>Do not arouse or awaken love<br>until the time is right.

### Solomon Arrives on His Wedding Day

<sup>6</sup> Who is this coming up from the wilderness<br>like a column of smoke,<br>scented with myrrh and frankincense<br>from all the spices of the merchant?<br>
<sup>7</sup> Behold, it is Solomon’s carriage,<sup>a</sup><br>escorted by sixty of the mightiest men of Israel.<br>
<sup>8</sup> All are skilled with the sword,<br>experienced in warfare.<br>Each has his sword at his side<br>prepared for the terror of the night.

<sup>9</sup> King Solomon has made his carriage<br>out of the timber of Lebanon.<br>
<sup>10</sup> He has made its posts of silver,<br>its base of gold, its seat of purple fabric.<br>Its interior is inlaid with love<br>by the daughters of Jerusalem.

<sup>11</sup> Come out, O daughters of Zion,<br>and gaze at King Solomon,<br>wearing the crown with which his mother crowned him<br>on the day of his wedding—<br>the day of his heart’s rejoicing.

> - <sup>a</sup> 7. That is, the couch on which servants carry a king

## Chapter 4

### Solomon Admires His Bride


### The Bridegroom

<sup>1</sup> How beautiful you are, my darling—<br>how very beautiful!<br>Your eyes are like doves<br>behind your veil.<br>Your hair is like a flock of goats<br>streaming down Mount Gilead.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Your teeth are like a flock of newly shorn sheep<br>coming up from the washing;<br>each has its twin,<br>and not one of them is lost.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Your lips are like a scarlet ribbon,<br>and your mouth is lovely.<br>Your brow behind your veil<br>is like a slice of pomegranate.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Your neck is like the tower of David,<br>built with rows of stones;<br>on it hang a thousand shields,<br>all of them shields of warriors.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Your breasts are like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle grazing among the lilies.

<sup>6</sup> Before the day breaks and the shadows flee,<br>I will make my way<br>to the mountain of myrrh<br>and to the hill of frankincense.<br>
<sup>7</sup> You are altogether beautiful, my darling;<br>in you there is no flaw.

<sup>8</sup> Come with me from Lebanon, my bride,<br>come with me from Lebanon!<br>Descend<sup>a</sup> from the peak of Amana,<br>from the summits of Senir and Hermon,<br>from the dens of the lions,<br>from the mountains of the leopards.<br>
<sup>9</sup> You have captured my heart,<br>my sister, my bride;<br>you have stolen my heart with one glance of your eyes,<br>with one jewel of your neck.<br>
<sup>10</sup> How delightful is your love,<br>my sister, my bride!<br>Your love is much better than wine,<br>and the fragrance of your perfume than all spices.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Your lips, my bride,<br>drip sweetness like the honeycomb;<br>honey and milk are under your tongue,<br>and the fragrance of your garments<br>is like the aroma of Lebanon.

<sup>12</sup> My sister, my bride, you are a garden locked up,<br>a spring enclosed, a fountain sealed.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Your branches are an orchard of pomegranates<br>with the choicest of fruits, with henna and nard,<br>
<sup>14</sup> with nard and saffron, with calamus and cinnamon,<br>with every kind of frankincense tree,<br>with myrrh and aloes,<br>with all the finest spices.<br>
<sup>15</sup> You are a garden spring,<br>a well of fresh water<sup>b</sup><br>flowing down from Lebanon.

### The Bride

<sup>16</sup> Awake, O north wind,<br>and come, O south wind.<br>Breathe on my garden<br>and spread the fragrance of its spices.<br>Let my beloved come into his garden<br>and taste its choicest fruits.

> - <sup>a</sup> 8. Or _Look down_
> - <sup>b</sup> 15. Or _flowing water_ or _living water_

## Chapter 5

### The Bride and Her Beloved


### The Bridegroom

<sup>1</sup> I have come to my garden, my sister, my bride;<br>I have gathered my myrrh with my spice.<br>I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey;<br>I have drunk my wine with my milk.
### The Friends


Eat, O friends, and drink;<br>drink freely, O beloved.

### The Bride

<sup>2</sup> I sleep, but my heart is awake.<br>A sound! My beloved is knocking:<br>“Open to me, my sister, my darling,<br>my dove, my flawless one.<br>My head is drenched with dew,<br>my hair with the dampness of the night.”

<sup>3</sup> I have taken off my robe—<br>must I put it back on?<br>I have washed my feet—<br>must I soil them again?<br>
<sup>4</sup> My beloved put his hand to the latch;<br>my heart pounded for him.<br>
<sup>5</sup> I rose up to open for my beloved.<br>My hands dripped with myrrh,<br>my fingers with flowing myrrh<br>on the handles of the bolt.<br>
<sup>6</sup> I opened for my beloved,<br>but he had turned and gone.<br>My heart sank at his departure.<br>I sought him but did not find him.<br>I called, but he did not answer.

<sup>7</sup> I encountered the watchmen on their rounds of the city.<br>They beat me and bruised me;<br>they took away my cloak,<br>those guardians of the walls.<br>
<sup>8</sup> O daughters of Jerusalem, I adjure you,<br>if you find my beloved,<br>tell him I am sick with love.

### The Friends

<sup>9</sup> How is your beloved better than others,<br>O most beautiful among women?<br>How is your beloved better than another,<br>that you charge us so?

### The Bride

<sup>10</sup> My beloved is dazzling and ruddy,<br>outstanding among ten thousand.<br>
<sup>11</sup> His head is purest gold;<br>his hair is wavy and black as a raven.<br>
<sup>12</sup> His eyes are like doves<br>beside the streams of water,<br>bathed in milk<br>and mounted like jewels.<br>
<sup>13</sup> His cheeks are like beds of spice,<br>towers of perfume.<br>His lips are like lilies,<br>dripping with flowing myrrh.<br>
<sup>14</sup> His arms are rods of gold<br>set with beryl.<br>His body is polished ivory<br>bedecked with sapphires.<br>
<sup>15</sup> His legs are pillars of marble<br>set on bases of pure gold.<br>His appearance is like Lebanon,<br>as majestic as the cedars.<br>
<sup>16</sup> His mouth<sup>a</sup> is most sweet;<br>he is altogether lovely.<br>This is my beloved, and this is my friend,<br>O daughters of Jerusalem.

> - <sup>a</sup> 16. Hebrew _palate_

## Chapter 6

### Together in the Garden


### The Friends

<sup>1</sup> Where has your beloved gone,<br>O most beautiful among women?<br>Which way has he turned?<br>We will seek him with you.

### The Bride

<sup>2</sup> My beloved has gone down to his garden,<br>to the beds of spices,<br>to pasture his flock in the gardens<br>and to gather lilies.<br>
<sup>3</sup> I belong to my beloved and he belongs to me;<br>he pastures his flock among the lilies.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>4</sup> You are as beautiful, my darling, as Tirzah,<br>as lovely as Jerusalem,<br>as majestic as troops with banners.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Turn your eyes away from me,<br>for they have overcome me.<br>Your hair is like a flock of goats<br>streaming down from Gilead.<br>
<sup>6</sup> Your teeth are like a flock of sheep<br>coming up from the washing;<br>each has its twin,<br>and not one of them is lost.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Your brow behind your veil<br>is like a slice of pomegranate.

<sup>8</sup> There are sixty queens and eighty concubines,<br>and maidens without number,<br>
<sup>9</sup> but my dove, my perfect one, is unique,<br>the favorite of the mother who bore her.<br>The maidens see her and call her blessed;<br>the queens and concubines sing her praises.

### The Friends

<sup>10</sup> Who is this who shines like the dawn,<br>as fair as the moon,<br>as bright as the sun,<br>as majestic as the stars in procession?

### The Bridegroom

<sup>11</sup> I went down to the walnut grove<br>to see the blossoms of the valley,<br>to see if the vines were budding<br>or the pomegranates were in bloom.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Before I realized it, my desire had set me<br>among the royal chariots of my people.<sup>a</sup>

### The Friends

<sup>13</sup> Come back, come back, O Shulammite!<br>Come back, come back, that we may gaze upon you.
### The Bridegroom


Why do you look at the Shulammite,<br>as on the dance of Mahanaim?<sup>b</sup>

> - <sup>a</sup> 12. Or _among the chariots of Amminadab_
> - <sup>b</sup> 13. Or _the dance of the two camps_

## Chapter 7

### Admiration by the Bridegroom

<sup>1</sup> How beautiful are your sandaled feet,<br>O daughter of the prince!<br>The curves of your thighs are like jewels,<br>the handiwork of a master.<br>
<sup>2</sup> Your navel is a rounded goblet;<br>it never lacks blended wine.<br>Your waist is a mound of wheat<br>encircled by the lilies.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Your breasts are like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Your neck is like a tower<br>made of ivory;<br>your eyes are like the pools of Heshbon<br>by the gate of Bath-rabbim;<br>your nose is like the tower of Lebanon,<br>facing toward Damascus.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Your head crowns you like Mount Carmel,<br>the hair of your head like purple threads;<br>the king is captured in your tresses.

<sup>6</sup> How fair and pleasant you are,<br>O love, with your delights!<br>
<sup>7</sup> Your stature is like a palm tree;<br>your breasts are clusters of fruit.<br>
<sup>8</sup> I said, “I will climb the palm tree;<br>I will take hold of its fruit.”<br>May your breasts be like clusters of the vine,<br>the fragrance of your breath like apples,<br>
<sup>9</sup> and your mouth<sup>a</sup> like the finest wine.
### The Bride


May it flow smoothly to my beloved,<br>gliding gently over lips and teeth.<sup>b</sup>

<sup>10</sup> I belong to my beloved,<br>and his desire is for me.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Come, my beloved,<br>let us go to the countryside;<br>let us spend the night among the wildflowers.<sup>c</sup>

<sup>12</sup> Let us go early to the vineyards<br>to see if the vine has budded,<br>if the blossom has opened,<br>if the pomegranates are in bloom—<br>there I will give you my love.

<sup>13</sup> The mandrakes send forth a fragrance,<br>and at our door is every delicacy,<br>new as well as old,<br>that I have treasured up for you, my beloved.

> - <sup>a</sup> 9. Hebrew _palate_
> - <sup>b</sup> 9. LXX, Syriac, and Vulgate; Hebrew _gliding gently over lips as we sleep_
> - <sup>c</sup> 11. Or _among the henna blossoms_ or _in the villages_

## Chapter 8

### Longing for Her Beloved

<sup>1</sup> O that you were to me like a brother<br>who nursed at my mother’s breasts!<br>If I found you outdoors, I would kiss you,<br>and no one would despise me.<br>
<sup>2</sup> I would lead you and bring you<br>to the house of my mother who taught me.<br>I would give you spiced wine to drink,<br>the nectar of my pomegranates.

<sup>3</sup> His left hand is under my head,<br>and his right arm embraces me.<br>
<sup>4</sup> O daughters of Jerusalem, I adjure you:<br>Do not arouse or awaken love<br>until the time is right.

### The Friends

<sup>5</sup> Who is this coming up from the wilderness,<br>leaning on her beloved?
### The Bride


I roused you under the apple tree;<br>there your mother conceived you;<br>there she travailed and brought you forth.

<sup>6</sup> Set me as a seal over your heart,<br>as a seal upon your arm.<br>For love is as strong as death,<br>its jealousy<sup>a</sup> as unrelenting as Sheol.<br>Its sparks are fiery flames,<br>the fiercest blaze of all.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Mighty waters cannot quench love;<br>rivers cannot sweep it away.<br>If a man were to give all the wealth of his house for love,<br>his offer would be utterly scorned.

### The Friends

<sup>8</sup> We have a little sister,<br>and her breasts are not yet grown.<br>What shall we do for our sister<br>on the day she is spoken for?<br>
<sup>9</sup> If she is a wall,<br>we will build a tower of silver upon her.<br>If she is a door,<br>we will enclose her with panels of cedar.

### The Bride

<sup>10</sup> I am a wall,<br>and my breasts are like towers.<br>So I have become in his eyes<br>like one who brings peace.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Solomon had a vineyard in Baal-hamon.<br>He leased it to the tenants.<br>For its fruit, each was to bring<br>a thousand shekels of silver.<sup>b</sup><br>
<sup>12</sup> But my own vineyard is mine to give;<br>the thousand shekels are for you, O Solomon,<br>and two hundred are for those who tend its fruit.

### The Bridegroom

<sup>13</sup> You who dwell in the gardens,<br>my companions are listening for your voice.<br>Let me hear it!

### The Bride

<sup>14</sup> Come away, my beloved,<br>and be like a gazelle<br>or a young stag<br>on the mountains of spices.

> - <sup>a</sup> 6. Or _passion_
> - <sup>b</sup> 11. Hebrew _a thousand of silver_; that is, approximately 25.1 pounds or 11.4 kilograms of silver
