## Chapter 1

### How Lonely Lies the City!<sup>a</sup>

<sup>1</sup> How<sup>b</sup> lonely lies the city,<br>once so full of people!<br>She who was great among the nations<br>has become a widow.<br>The princess of the provinces<br>has become a slave.

<sup>2</sup> She weeps aloud in the night,<br>with tears upon her cheeks.<br>Among all her lovers<br>there is no one to comfort her.<br>All her friends have betrayed her;<br>they have become her enemies.

<sup>3</sup> Judah has gone into exile<br>under affliction and harsh slavery;<br>she dwells among the nations<br>but finds no place to rest.<br>All her pursuers have overtaken her<br>in the midst of her distress.

<sup>4</sup> The roads to Zion mourn,<br>because no one comes to her appointed feasts.<br>All her gates are deserted;<br>her priests groan,<br>her maidens grieve,<br>and she herself is bitter with anguish.

<sup>5</sup> Her foes have become her masters;<br>her enemies are at ease.<br>For the LORD has brought her grief<br>because of her many transgressions.<br>Her children have gone away<br>as captives before the enemy.

<sup>6</sup> All the splendor has departed<br>from the Daughter of Zion.<br>Her princes are like deer<br>that find no pasture;<br>they lack the strength to flee<br>in the face of the hunter.

<sup>7</sup> In the days of her affliction and wandering<br>Jerusalem remembers all the treasures<br>that were hers in days of old.<br>When her people fell into enemy hands<br>she received no help.<br>Her enemies looked upon her,<br>laughing at her downfall.

<sup>8</sup> Jerusalem has sinned greatly;<br>therefore she has become an object of scorn.<br>All who honored her now despise her,<br>for they have seen her nakedness;<sup>c</sup><br>she herself groans and turns away.

<sup>9</sup> Her uncleanness stains her skirts;<br>she did not consider her end.<br>Her downfall was astounding;<br>there was no one to comfort her.<br>Look, O LORD, on my affliction,<br>for the enemy has triumphed!

<sup>10</sup> The adversary has seized<br>all her treasures.<br>For she has seen the nations<br>enter her sanctuary—<br>those You had forbidden<br>to enter Your assembly.

<sup>11</sup> All her people groan<br>as they search for bread.<br>They have traded their treasures for food<br>to keep themselves alive.<br>Look, O LORD, and consider,<br>for I have become despised.

<sup>12</sup> Is this nothing to you, all you who pass by?<br>Look around and see!<br>Is there any sorrow like mine,<br>which was inflicted on me,<br>which the LORD made me suffer<br>on the day of His fierce anger?

<sup>13</sup> He sent fire from on high,<br>and it overpowered my bones.<br>He spread a net for my feet<br>and turned me back.<br>He made me desolate,<br>faint all the day long.

<sup>14</sup> My transgressions are bound into a yoke,<sup>d</sup><br>knit together by His hand;<br>they are draped over my neck,<br>and the Lord has broken my strength.<br>He has delivered me into the hands<br>of those I cannot withstand.

<sup>15</sup> The Lord has rejected<br>all the mighty men in my midst;<br>He has summoned an army against me<sup>e</sup><br>to crush my young warriors.<br>Like grapes in a winepress,<br>the Lord has trampled the Virgin Daughter of Judah.

<sup>16</sup> For these things I weep;<br>my eyes flow with tears.<br>For there is no one nearby to comfort me,<br>no one to revive my soul.<br>My children are destitute<br>because the enemy has prevailed.

<sup>17</sup> Zion stretches out her hands,<br>but there is no one to comfort her.<br>The LORD has decreed against Jacob<br>that his neighbors become his foes.<br>Jerusalem has become<br>an unclean thing among them.

<sup>18</sup> The LORD is righteous,<br>yet I rebelled against His command.<br>Listen, all you people;<br>look upon my suffering.<br>My young men and maidens<br>have gone into captivity.

<sup>19</sup> I called out to my lovers,<br>but they have betrayed me.<br>My priests and elders<br>perished in the city<br>while they searched for food<br>to keep themselves alive.

<sup>20</sup> See, O LORD, how distressed I am!<br>I am churning within;<br>my heart is pounding within me,<br>for I have been most rebellious.<br>Outside, the sword bereaves;<br>inside, there is death.

<sup>21</sup> People have heard my groaning,<br>but there is no one to comfort me.<br>All my enemies have heard of my trouble;<br>they are glad that You have caused it.<br>May You bring the day You have announced,<br>so that they may become like me.

<sup>22</sup> Let all their wickedness come before You,<br>and deal with them<br>as You have dealt with me<br>because of all my transgressions.<br>For my groans are many,<br>and my heart is faint.

> - <sup>a</sup> Heading. 2 Kings 24:10–17
> - <sup>b</sup> 1. This chapter is an acrostic poem, each verse beginning with the successive letters of the Hebrew alphabet.
> - <sup>c</sup> 8. Or _her shame_
> - <sup>d</sup> 14. Most Hebrew manuscripts; other Hebrew manuscripts and LXX _He kept watch over my sins_
> - <sup>e</sup> 15. Or _has set a time for me_

## Chapter 2

### God’s Anger over Jerusalem

<sup>1</sup> How<sup>a</sup> the Lord has covered the Daughter of Zion<br>with the cloud of His anger!<br>He has cast the glory of Israel<br>from heaven to earth.<br>He has abandoned His footstool<br>in the day of His anger.

<sup>2</sup> Without pity the Lord has swallowed up<br>all the dwellings of Jacob.<br>In His wrath He has demolished<br>the fortified cities of the Daughter of Judah.<br>He brought to the ground and defiled<br>her kingdom and its princes.

<sup>3</sup> In fierce anger He has cut off<br>every horn<sup>b</sup> of Israel<br>and withdrawn His right hand<br>at the approach of the enemy.<br>He has burned in Jacob like a flaming fire<br>that consumes everything around it.

<sup>4</sup> He has bent His bow like an enemy;<br>His right hand is positioned.<br>Like a foe He has killed<br>all who were pleasing to the eye;<br>He has poured out His wrath like fire<br>on the tent of the Daughter of Zion.

<sup>5</sup> The Lord is like an enemy;<br>He has swallowed up Israel.<br>He has swallowed up all her palaces<br>and destroyed her strongholds.<br>He has multiplied mourning and lamentation<br>for the Daughter of Judah.

<sup>6</sup> He has laid waste His tabernacle like a garden booth;<br>He has destroyed His place of meeting.<br>The LORD has made Zion forget<br>her appointed feasts and Sabbaths.<br>In His fierce anger<br>He has despised both king and priest.

<sup>7</sup> The Lord has rejected His altar;<br>He has abandoned His sanctuary;<br>He has delivered the walls of her palaces<br>into the hand of the enemy.<br>They have raised a shout in the house of the LORD<br>as on the day of an appointed feast.

<sup>8</sup> The LORD determined to destroy<br>the wall of the Daughter of Zion.<br>He stretched out a measuring line<br>and did not withdraw His hand from destroying.<br>He made the ramparts and walls lament;<br>together they waste away.

<sup>9</sup> Her gates have sunk into the ground;<br>He has destroyed and shattered their bars.<br>Her king and her princes are exiled among the nations,<br>the law is no more,<br>and even her prophets<br>find no vision from the LORD.

<sup>10</sup> The elders of the Daughter of Zion<br>sit on the ground in silence.<br>They have thrown dust on their heads<br>and put on sackcloth.<br>The young women of Jerusalem<br>have bowed their heads to the ground.

<sup>11</sup> My eyes fail from weeping;<br>I am churning within.<br>My heart is poured out in grief<br>over the destruction of the daughter of my people,<br>because children and infants faint<br>in the streets of the city.

<sup>12</sup> They cry out to their mothers:<br>“Where is the grain and wine?”<br>as they faint like the wounded<br>in the streets of the city,<br>as their lives fade away<br>in the arms of their mothers.

<sup>13</sup> What can I say for you?<br>To what can I compare you,<br>O Daughter of Jerusalem?<br>To what can I liken you,<br>that I may console you,<br>O Virgin Daughter of Zion?<br>For your wound is as deep as the sea.<br>Who can ever heal you?

<sup>14</sup> The visions of your prophets<br>were empty and deceptive;<br>they did not expose your guilt<br>to ward off your captivity.<br>The burdens they envisioned for you<br>were empty and misleading.

<sup>15</sup> All who pass by<br>clap their hands at you in scorn.<br>They hiss and shake their heads<br>at the Daughter of Jerusalem:<br>“Is this the city that was called<br>the perfection of beauty,<br>the joy of all the earth?”

<sup>16</sup> All your enemies<br>open their mouths against you.<br>They hiss and gnash their teeth,<br>saying, “We have swallowed her up.<br>This is the day for which we have waited.<br>We have lived to see it!”

<sup>17</sup> The LORD has done what He planned;<br>He has accomplished His decree,<br>which He ordained in days of old;<br>He has overthrown you without pity.<br>He has let the enemy gloat over you<br>and exalted the horn<sup>c</sup> of your foes.

<sup>18</sup> The hearts of the people<br>cry out to the Lord.<sup>d</sup><br>O wall of the Daughter of Zion,<br>let your tears run down like a river<br>day and night.<br>Give yourself no relief,<br>and your eyes no rest.

<sup>19</sup> Arise, cry out in the night<br>from the first watch of the night.<sup>e</sup><br>Pour out your heart like water<br>in the presence of the Lord.<br>Lift up your hands to Him<br>for the lives of your children<br>who are fainting from hunger<br>on the corner of every street.

<sup>20</sup> Look, O LORD, and consider:<br>Whom have You ever treated like this?<br>Should women eat their offspring,<br>the infants they have nurtured?<br>Should priests and prophets be killed<br>in the sanctuary of the Lord?

<sup>21</sup> Both young and old lie together<br>in the dust of the streets.<br>My young men and maidens<br>have fallen by the sword.<br>You have slain them in the day of Your anger;<br>You have slaughtered them without compassion.

<sup>22</sup> You summoned my terrors on every side,<br>as for the day of an appointed feast.<br>In the day of the LORD’s anger<br>no one escaped or survived;<br>my enemy has destroyed<br>those I nurtured and reared.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. This chapter is an acrostic poem, each verse beginning with the successive letters of the Hebrew alphabet.
> - <sup>b</sup> 3. Or _all the strength_
> - <sup>c</sup> 17. Or _the strength_
> - <sup>d</sup> 18. Literally _Their heart cries out to the Lord._
> - <sup>e</sup> 19. That is, between six and nine at night

## Chapter 3

### The Prophet’s Afflictions

<sup>1</sup> I<sup>a</sup> am the man who has seen affliction<br>under the rod of God’s wrath.<br>
<sup>2</sup> He has driven me away and made me walk<br>in darkness instead of light.<br>
<sup>3</sup> Indeed, He keeps turning His hand<br>against me all day long.

<sup>4</sup> He has worn away my flesh and skin;<br>He has shattered my bones.<br>
<sup>5</sup> He has besieged me and surrounded me<br>with bitterness and hardship.<br>
<sup>6</sup> He has made me dwell in darkness<br>like those dead for ages.

<sup>7</sup> He has walled me in so I cannot escape;<br>He has weighed me down with chains.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Even when I cry out and plead for help,<br>He shuts out my prayer.<br>
<sup>9</sup> He has barred my ways with cut stones;<br>He has made my paths crooked.

<sup>10</sup> He is a bear lying in wait,<br>a lion hiding in ambush.<br>
<sup>11</sup> He forced me off my path and tore me to pieces;<br>He left me without help.<br>
<sup>12</sup> He bent His bow<br>and set me as the target for His arrow.

<sup>13</sup> He pierced my kidneys<br>with His arrows.<br>
<sup>14</sup> I am a laughingstock to all my people;<br>they mock me in song all day long.<br>
<sup>15</sup> He has filled me with bitterness;<br>He has intoxicated me with wormwood.

<sup>16</sup> He has ground my teeth with gravel<br>and trampled me in the dust.<br>
<sup>17</sup> My soul has been deprived of peace;<br>I have forgotten what prosperity is.<br>
<sup>18</sup> So I say, “My strength has perished,<br>along with my hope from the LORD.”

### The Prophet’s Hope

<sup>19</sup> Remember my affliction and wandering,<br>the wormwood and the gall.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Surely my soul remembers<br>and is humbled within me.<br>
<sup>21</sup> Yet I call this to mind,<br>and therefore I have hope:

<sup>22</sup> Because of the loving devotion<sup>b</sup> of the LORD we are not consumed,<br>for His mercies never fail.<br>
<sup>23</sup> They are new every morning;<br>great is Your faithfulness!<br>
<sup>24</sup> “The LORD is my portion,” says my soul,<br>“therefore I will hope in Him.”

<sup>25</sup> The LORD is good to those who wait for Him,<br>to the soul who seeks Him.<br>
<sup>26</sup> It is good to wait quietly<br>for the salvation of the LORD.<br>
<sup>27</sup> It is good for a man to bear the yoke<br>while he is still young.

<sup>28</sup> Let him sit alone in silence,<br>for the LORD has laid it upon him.<br>
<sup>29</sup> Let him bury his face in the dust—<br>perhaps there is still hope.<br>
<sup>30</sup> Let him offer his cheek to the one who would strike him;<br>let him be filled with reproach.

<sup>31</sup> For the Lord will not<br>cast us off forever.<br>
<sup>32</sup> Even if He causes grief, He will show compassion<br>according to His abundant loving devotion.<br>
<sup>33</sup> For He does not willingly afflict<br>or grieve the sons of men.

<sup>34</sup> To crush underfoot<br>all the prisoners of the land,<br>
<sup>35</sup> to deny a man justice<br>before the Most High,<br>
<sup>36</sup> to subvert a man in his lawsuit—<br>of these the Lord does not approve.

### God’s Justice

<sup>37</sup> Who has spoken and it came to pass,<br>unless the Lord has ordained it?<br>
<sup>38</sup> Do not both adversity and good<br>come from the mouth of the Most High?<br>
<sup>39</sup> Why should any mortal man complain,<br>in view of his sins?

<sup>40</sup> Let us examine and test our ways,<br>and turn back to the LORD.<br>
<sup>41</sup> Let us lift up our hearts and hands<br>to God in heaven:<br>
<sup>42</sup> “We have sinned and rebelled;<br>You have not forgiven.”

<sup>43</sup> You have covered Yourself in anger and pursued us;<br>You have killed without pity.<br>
<sup>44</sup> You have covered Yourself with a cloud<br>that no prayer can pass through.<br>
<sup>45</sup> You have made us scum and refuse<br>among the nations.

<sup>46</sup> All our enemies<br>open their mouths against us.<br>
<sup>47</sup> Panic and pitfall have come upon us—<br>devastation and destruction.<br>
<sup>48</sup> Streams of tears flow from my eyes<br>over the destruction of the daughter of my people.

<sup>49</sup> My eyes overflow unceasingly,<br>without relief,<br>
<sup>50</sup> until the LORD<br>looks down from heaven and sees.<br>
<sup>51</sup> My eyes bring grief to my soul<br>because of all the daughters of my city.

<sup>52</sup> Without cause my enemies<br>hunted me like a bird.<br>
<sup>53</sup> They dropped me alive into a pit<br>and cast stones upon me.<br>
<sup>54</sup> The waters flowed over my head,<br>and I thought I was going to die.

<sup>55</sup> I called on Your name, O LORD,<br>out of the depths of the Pit.<br>
<sup>56</sup> You heard my plea:<br>“Do not ignore my cry for relief.”<br>
<sup>57</sup> You drew near when I called on You;<br>You said, “Do not be afraid.”

<sup>58</sup> You defend my cause, O Lord;<br>You redeem my life.<br>
<sup>59</sup> You have seen, O LORD, the wrong done to me;<br>vindicate my cause!<br>
<sup>60</sup> You have seen all their malice,<br>all their plots against me.

<sup>61</sup> O LORD, You have heard their insults,<br>all their plots against me—<br>
<sup>62</sup> the slander and murmuring of my assailants<br>against me all day long.<br>
<sup>63</sup> When they sit and when they rise,<br>see how they mock me in song.

<sup>64</sup> You will pay them back what they deserve, O LORD,<br>according to the work of their hands.<br>
<sup>65</sup> Put a veil of anguish over their hearts;<br>may Your curse be upon them!<br>
<sup>66</sup> You will pursue them in anger and exterminate them<br>from under Your heavens, O LORD.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. This chapter is an acrostic poem, each 3–verse stanza beginning with the successive letters of the Hebrew alphabet.
> - <sup>b</sup> 22. Forms of the Hebrew _chesed_ are translated here and in most cases throughout the Scriptures as _loving devotion_; the range of meaning includes _love_, _goodness_, _kindness_, _faithfulness_, and _mercy_, as well as _loyalty to a covenant_.

## Chapter 4

### The Distress of Zion

<sup>1</sup> How<sup>a</sup> the gold has become tarnished,<br>the pure gold has become dull!<br>The gems of the temple lie scattered<br>on every street corner.

<sup>2</sup> How the precious sons of Zion,<br>once worth their weight in pure gold,<br>are now esteemed as jars of clay,<br>the work of a potter’s hands!

<sup>3</sup> Even jackals<sup>b</sup> offer their breasts<br>to nurse their young,<br>but the daughter of my people has become cruel,<br>like an ostrich in the wilderness.

<sup>4</sup> The nursing infant’s tongue<br>clings in thirst to the roof of his mouth.<br>Little children beg for bread,<br>but no one gives them any.

<sup>5</sup> Those who once ate delicacies<br>are destitute in the streets;<br>those brought up in crimson<br>huddle in ash heaps.

<sup>6</sup> The punishment<sup>c</sup> of the daughter of my people<br>is greater than that of Sodom,<br>which was overthrown in an instant<br>without a hand turned to help her.

<sup>7</sup> Her dignitaries were brighter than snow,<br>whiter than milk;<br>their bodies were more ruddy than rubies,<br>their appearance<sup>d</sup> like sapphires.<sup>e</sup>

<sup>8</sup> But now their appearance is blacker than soot;<br>they are not recognized in the streets.<br>Their skin has shriveled on their bones;<br>it has become as dry as a stick.

<sup>9</sup> Those slain by the sword are better off<br>than those who die of hunger,<br>who waste away, pierced with pain<br>because the fields lack produce.

<sup>10</sup> The hands of compassionate women<br>have cooked their own children,<br>who became their food<br>in the destruction of the daughter of my people.

<sup>11</sup> The LORD has exhausted His wrath;<br>He has poured out His fierce anger;<br>He has kindled a fire in Zion,<br>and it has consumed her foundations.

<sup>12</sup> The kings of the earth did not believe,<br>nor any people of the world,<br>that an enemy or a foe<br>could enter the gates of Jerusalem.

<sup>13</sup> But this was for the sins of her prophets<br>and the guilt of her priests,<br>who shed the blood of the righteous<br>in her midst.

<sup>14</sup> They wandered blind in the streets,<br>defiled by this blood,<br>so that no one dared<br>to touch their garments.

<sup>15</sup> “Go away! Unclean!”<br>men shouted at them.<br>“Away, away! Do not touch us!”<br>So they fled and wandered.<br>Among the nations it was said,<br>“They can stay here no longer.”

<sup>16</sup> The presence of the LORD has scattered them;<br>He regards them no more.<br>The priests are shown no honor;<br>the elders find no favor.

<sup>17</sup> All the while our eyes were failing<br>as we looked in vain for help.<br>We watched from our towers<br>for a nation that could not save us.

<sup>18</sup> They stalked our every step,<br>so that we could not walk in our streets.<br>Our end drew near, our time ran out,<br>for our end had come!

<sup>19</sup> Those who chased us were swifter<br>than the eagles in the sky;<br>they pursued us over the mountains<br>and ambushed us in the wilderness.

<sup>20</sup> The LORD’s anointed, the breath of our life,<br>was captured in their pits.<br>We had said of him,<br>“Under his shadow we will live among the nations.”

<sup>21</sup> So rejoice and be glad, O Daughter of Edom,<br>you who dwell in the land of Uz.<br>Yet the cup will pass to you as well;<br>you will get drunk and expose yourself.

<sup>22</sup> O Daughter of Zion, your punishment is complete;<br>He will not prolong your exile.<sup>f</sup><br>But He will punish your iniquity, O Daughter of Edom;<br>He will expose your sins.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. This chapter is an acrostic poem, each verse beginning with the successive letters of the Hebrew alphabet.
> - <sup>b</sup> 3. Or _serpents_ or _dragons_
> - <sup>c</sup> 6. Or _iniquity_
> - <sup>d</sup> 7. Or _their polishing_ or _their hair_
> - <sup>e</sup> 7. Hebrew _lapis lazuli_
> - <sup>f</sup> 22. Or _He will not exile you again_

## Chapter 5

### A Prayer for Restoration

<sup>1</sup> Remember, O LORD, what has happened to us.<br>Look and see our disgrace!<br>
<sup>2</sup> Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers,<br>our houses to foreigners.<br>
<sup>3</sup> We have become fatherless orphans;<br>our mothers are widows.<br>
<sup>4</sup> We must buy the water we drink;<br>our wood comes at a price.<br>
<sup>5</sup> We are closely pursued;<br>we are weary and find no rest.

<sup>6</sup> We submitted to Egypt and Assyria<br>to get enough bread.<br>
<sup>7</sup> Our fathers sinned and are no more,<br>but we bear their punishment.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Slaves rule over us;<br>there is no one to deliver us from their hands.<br>
<sup>9</sup> We get our bread at the risk of our lives<br>because of the sword in the wilderness.<br>
<sup>10</sup> Our skin is as hot as an oven<br>with fever from our hunger.

<sup>11</sup> Women have been ravished in Zion,<br>virgins in the cities of Judah.<br>
<sup>12</sup> Princes have been hung up by their hands;<br>elders receive no respect.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Young men toil at millstones;<br>boys stagger under loads of wood.<br>
<sup>14</sup> The elders have left the city gate;<br>the young men have stopped their music.<br>
<sup>15</sup> Joy has left our hearts;<br>our dancing has turned to mourning.<br>
<sup>16</sup> The crown has fallen from our head.<br>Woe to us, for we have sinned!

<sup>17</sup> Because of this, our hearts are faint;<br>because of these, our eyes grow dim—<br>
<sup>18</sup> because of Mount Zion, which lies desolate,<br>patrolled by foxes.

<sup>19</sup> You, O LORD, reign forever;<br>Your throne endures from generation to generation.<br>
<sup>20</sup> Why have You forgotten us forever?<br>Why have You forsaken us for so long?<br>
<sup>21</sup> Restore us to Yourself, O LORD, so we may return;<br>renew our days as of old,<br>
<sup>22</sup> unless You have utterly rejected us<br>and remain angry with us beyond measure.
