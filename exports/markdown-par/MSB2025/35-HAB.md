## Chapter 1

### Habakkuk’s First Complaint

<sup>1</sup> This is the burden that Habakkuk the prophet received in a vision:

<sup>2</sup> How long, O LORD, must I call for help<br>but You do not hear,<br>or cry out to You, “Violence!”<br>but You do not save?<br>
<sup>3</sup> Why do You make me see iniquity?<br>Why do You tolerate wrongdoing?<br>Destruction and violence are before me.<br>Strife is ongoing, and conflict abounds.<br>
<sup>4</sup> Therefore the law is paralyzed,<br>and justice never goes forth.<br>For the wicked hem in the righteous,<br>so that justice is perverted.

### The LORD’s Answer

<sup>5</sup> “Look at the nations and observe—<br>be utterly astounded!<sup>a</sup><br>For I am doing a work in your days<br>that you would never believe<br>even if someone told you.<sup>b</sup><br>
<sup>6</sup> For behold, I am raising up the Chaldeans<sup>c</sup>—<br>that ruthless and impetuous nation<br>which marches through the breadth of the earth<br>to seize dwellings not their own.<br>
<sup>7</sup> They are dreaded and feared;<br>from themselves they derive justice and sovereignty.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Their horses are swifter than leopards,<br>fiercer than wolves of the night.<br>Their horsemen charge ahead,<br>and their cavalry comes from afar.<br>They fly like a vulture,<br>swooping down to devour.<br>
<sup>9</sup> All of them come bent on violence;<br>their hordes advance like the east wind;<br>they gather prisoners like sand.<br>
<sup>10</sup> They scoff at kings<br>and make rulers an object of scorn.<br>They laugh at every fortress<br>and build up siege ramps to seize it.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Then they sweep by like the wind<br>and pass through.<br>They are guilty;<br>their own strength is their god.”

### Habakkuk’s Second Complaint<sup>d</sup>

<sup>12</sup> Are You not from everlasting,<br>O LORD, my God, my Holy One?<br>We will not die.<br>O LORD, You have appointed them<br>to execute judgment;<br>O Rock, You have established them<br>for correction.<br>
<sup>13</sup> Your eyes are too pure to look upon evil,<br>and You cannot tolerate wrongdoing.<br>So why do You tolerate the faithless?<br>Why are You silent<br>while the wicked swallow up<br>those more righteous than themselves?<br>
<sup>14</sup> You have made men like the fish of the sea,<br>like creeping things that have no ruler.<br>
<sup>15</sup> The foe pulls all of them up<sup>e</sup> with a hook;<br>he catches them in his dragnet,<br>and gathers them in his fishing net;<br>so he rejoices gladly.<br>
<sup>16</sup> Therefore he sacrifices to his dragnet<br>and burns incense to his fishing net,<br>for by these things his portion is sumptuous<br>and his food is rich.<br>
<sup>17</sup> Will he, therefore, empty his net<br>and continue to slay nations without mercy?

> - <sup>a</sup> 5. LXX _Look, you scoffers, wonder and perish!_
> - <sup>b</sup> 5. Cited in Acts 13:41
> - <sup>c</sup> 6. That is, the Babylonians
> - <sup>d</sup> Heading. Psalm 11:1–7
> - <sup>e</sup> 15. Literally _He pulls all of them up_

## Chapter 2

### The LORD Answers Again

<sup>1</sup> I will stand at my guard post<br>and station myself on the ramparts.<br>I will watch to see what He will say to me,<br>and how I should answer when corrected.

<sup>2</sup> Then the LORD answered me:

“Write down this vision<br>and clearly inscribe it on tablets,<br>so that a herald may run with it.<br>
<sup>3</sup> For the vision awaits an appointed time;<br>it testifies of the end and does not lie.<br>Though it lingers, wait for it,<br>since it will surely come and will not delay.<sup>a</sup><br>
<sup>4</sup> Look at the proud one; his soul is not upright<sup>b</sup>—<br>but the righteous will live by faith<sup>c</sup>—<br>
<sup>5</sup> and wealth<sup>d</sup> indeed betrays him.<br>He is an arrogant man never at rest.<br>He enlarges his appetite like Sheol,<br>and like Death, he is never satisfied.<br>He gathers all the nations to himself<br>and collects all the peoples as his own.

### Woe to the Chaldeans

<sup>6</sup> Will not all of these take up a taunt against him,<br>speaking with mockery and derision:

‘Woe to him who amasses what is not his<br>and makes himself rich with many loans!<br>How long will this go on?’<br>
<sup>7</sup> Will not your creditors suddenly arise<br>and those who disturb you awaken?<br>Then you will become their prey.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Because you have plundered many nations,<br>the remnant of the people will plunder you—<br>because of your bloodshed against man<br>and your violence against the land, the city,<br>and all their dwellers.

<sup>9</sup> Woe to him who builds his house<br>by unjust gain,<br>to place his nest on high<br>and escape the hand of disaster!<br>
<sup>10</sup> You have plotted shame for your house<br>by cutting off many peoples<br>and forfeiting your life.<sup>e</sup><br>
<sup>11</sup> For the stones will cry out from the wall,<br>and the rafters will echo it from the woodwork.

<sup>12</sup> Woe to him who builds a city with bloodshed<br>and establishes a town by iniquity!<br>
<sup>13</sup> Is it not indeed from the LORD of Hosts<br>that the labor of the people only feeds the fire,<br>and the nations weary themselves in vain?<br>
<sup>14</sup> For the earth will be filled<br>with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD<br>as the waters cover the sea.

<sup>15</sup> Woe to him who gives drink to his neighbors,<br>pouring it from the wineskin until they are drunk,<br>in order to gaze at their nakedness!<br>
<sup>16</sup> You will be filled with shame instead of glory.<br>You too must drink<br>and expose your uncircumcision!<sup>f</sup><br>The cup in the LORD’s right hand<br>will come around to you,<br>and utter disgrace will cover your glory.<br>
<sup>17</sup> For your violence against Lebanon will overwhelm you,<br>and the destruction of animals will terrify you,<br>because of your bloodshed against men<br>and your violence against the land, the city,<br>and all their dwellers.

<sup>18</sup> What use is an idol,<br>that a craftsman should carve it—<br>or an image,<br>a teacher of lies?<br>For its maker trusts in his own creation;<br>he makes idols that cannot speak.<br>
<sup>19</sup> Woe to him who says to wood, ‘Awake!’<br>or to silent stone, ‘Arise!’<br>Can it give guidance?<br>Behold, it is overlaid with gold and silver,<br>yet there is no breath in it at all.”

<sup>20</sup> But the LORD is in His holy temple;<br>let all the earth be silent before Him.

> - <sup>a</sup> 3. Or _Though He lingers, wait for Him, since He will surely come and will not delay_; see also LXX; cited in Hebrews 10:37.
> - <sup>b</sup> 4. LXX _If he should draw back, My soul has no pleasure in him_
> - <sup>c</sup> 4. Or _faithfulness_; cited in Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11, and Hebrews 10:38
> - <sup>d</sup> 5. DSS; MT _wine_
> - <sup>e</sup> 10. Literally _sinning against your soul_
> - <sup>f</sup> 16. DSS, LXX, and Syriac _and stagger_

## Chapter 3

### Habakkuk’s Prayer

<sup>1</sup> This is a prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, according to Shigionoth:<sup>a</sup>

<sup>2</sup> O LORD, I have heard the report of You;<br>I stand in awe, O LORD, of Your deeds.<br>Revive them in these years;<br>make them known in these years.<br>In Your wrath, remember mercy!

<sup>3</sup> God came from Teman,<br>and the Holy One from Mount Paran. _Selah_<sup>b</sup><br>His glory covered the heavens,<br>and His praise filled the earth.<br>
<sup>4</sup> His radiance was like the sunlight;<br>rays flashed from His hand,<br>where His power is hidden.<br>
<sup>5</sup> Plague went before Him,<br>and fever followed in His steps.<br>
<sup>6</sup> He stood and measured the earth;<br>He looked and startled the nations;<br>the ancient mountains crumbled;<br>the perpetual hills collapsed.<br>His ways are everlasting.

<sup>7</sup> I saw the tents of Cushan in distress;<br>the curtains of Midian were trembling.<br>
<sup>8</sup> Were You angry at the rivers, O LORD?<br>Was Your wrath against the streams?<br>Did You rage against the sea<br>when You rode on Your horses,<br>on Your chariots of salvation?<br>
<sup>9</sup> You brandished Your bow;<br>You called for many arrows. _Selah_<br>You split the earth with rivers.<br>
<sup>10</sup> The mountains saw You and quaked;<br>torrents of water swept by.<br>The deep roared with its voice<br>and lifted its hands on high.<br>
<sup>11</sup> Sun and moon stood still<br>in their places<br>at the flash of Your flying arrows,<br>at the brightness of Your shining spear.<br>
<sup>12</sup> You marched across the earth with fury;<br>You threshed the nations in wrath.<br>
<sup>13</sup> You went forth for the salvation of Your people,<br>to save Your anointed.<br>You crushed the head of the house of the wicked<br>and stripped him from head to toe. _Selah_<br>
<sup>14</sup> With his own spear You pierced his head,<br>when his warriors stormed out to scatter us,<br>gloating as though ready<br>to secretly devour the weak.<br>
<sup>15</sup> You trampled the sea with Your horses,<br>churning the great waters.

<sup>16</sup> I heard and trembled within;<br>my lips quivered at the sound.<br>Decay entered my bones;<br>I trembled where I stood.<br>Yet I must wait patiently for the day of distress<br>to come upon the people who invade us.

### Habakkuk Rejoices

<sup>17</sup> Though the fig tree does not bud<br>and no fruit is on the vines,<br>though the olive crop fails<br>and the fields produce no food,<br>though the sheep are cut off from the fold<br>and no cattle are in the stalls,<br>
<sup>18</sup> yet I will exult in the LORD;<br>I will rejoice in the God of my salvation!<br>
<sup>19</sup> GOD the Lord is my strength;<br>He makes my feet like those of a deer;<br>He makes me walk upon the heights!

For the choirmaster. With stringed instruments.

> - <sup>a</sup> 1. _Shigionoth_ is probably a musical term indicating the setting for the prayer.
> - <sup>b</sup> 3. _Selah_ or _Interlude_ is probably a musical or literary term; also in verses 9 and 13.
