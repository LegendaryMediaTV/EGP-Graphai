001:001 ¶ °{THE AUTHOR of this Letter was probably son of Alphaeus or Cleopas, surnamed “the less,” and “the just,” and “the Lord’s brother;” after the murder of James the son of Zebedee, he appears to have been foremost in Jerusalem, where he was martyred A.D. 68. This Letter (written about A.D. 67) has always formed a part of the Syriac Peshito Version, (which omits 2d Peter, 2d and 3d John, Jude and Revelation, executed before A.D. 200), and is found in all complete MSS. of the Greek Testament. It is quoted or referred to by Clement of Rome (A.D. 108), Hermas (A.D. 108), Origen (A.D. 230), Irenaeus (A.D. 178), Clement of Alexandria (A.D. 194), &c., but none of the Latin fathers before A.D. 300 quote it.␤It is an address by a Jewish believer, chiefly to his fellow-believers in Christ belonging to what is here called “the Twelve Tribes,” because remnants from each of these had mingled themselves with those of the two tribes, who had returned from Babylon. By the time this Letter was written hundreds of thousands of Jews had embraced the gospel, but the zeal of the unbelieving Jews and the practical errors of the Gnostics and Judaising false brethren rendered his position important and perilous, hence the pre-eminently practical character of his teaching in cautions, censures, and exhortations.␤It may be divided into three parts; first, ch. 1. 1–27; second, ch. 2. 1–5. 6; and thirdly, ch. 5. 7–20.} °{Chapter I. may be divided into seven parts; v. 1 introductory salutations; v. 2–8 call to rejoice because of trials, to ask of God unwaveringly; v. 9–12 exhortation to the low, the rich, and the tried; v. 13–15 whence temptations; v. 16–18 and every perfect gift come; v. 19–25 to be doers not hearers only; v. 26, 27 vain and true religion.} James, of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ a servant,°{Lit., bondsman} to the Twelve Tribes who are in the dispersion: Hail!°{Lit., “to rejoice!”}
001:002 ¶ All joy count it, my brethren, when ye may fall into temptations manifold;
001:003 knowing that the proof of your faith doth work endurance,°{Lit., “remaining under.”}
001:004 and let the endurance have a perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire—in nothing lacking;
001:005 ¶ and if any of you do lack wisdom, let him ask from God, who is giving to all liberally,°{Lit., simply (or with simplicity)} and not reproaching, and it shall be given to him;
001:006 and let him ask in faith, nothing doubting, for he who is doubting hath been like a wave°{Lit., washer} of the sea, driven by wind and tossed,
001:007 for let not that man suppose that he shall receive anything from the Lord—
001:008 a two-souled man is unstable in all his ways.
001:009 And let the brother who is low rejoice in his exaltation,
001:010 and the rich in his becoming low, because as a flower of grass he shall pass away;
001:011 for the sun did rise with the burning heat,°{Lit., hot-wind} and did wither the grass, and the flower of it fell, and the grace of its appearance°{Lit., face} did perish, so also the rich in his way shall fade away!
001:012 ¶ Happy the man who doth endure temptation, because, becoming approved, he shall receive the crown of the life, which the Lord did promise to those loving Him.
001:013 Let no one say, being tempted°{Lit., tried, i.e. with evil}—“From God I am tempted,” for God is not tempted of evil, and Himself doth tempt no one,
001:014 and each one is tempted, by his own desires being led away and enticed,°{Or, bribed by himself}
001:015 afterward the desire having conceived, doth give birth to sin, and the sin having been perfected, doth bring forth death.
001:016 Be not led astray, my brethren beloved;
001:017 every good giving, and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of the lights, with whom is no variation, or shadow of turning;
001:018 having counselled, He did beget us with a word of truth, for our being a certain firstfruit of His creatures.
001:019 ¶ So then, my brethren beloved, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger,
001:020 for the wrath of a man the righteousness of God doth not work;
001:021 wherefore having put aside all filthiness and superabundance of evil, in meekness be receiving the engrafted word, that is able to save your souls;
001:022 and become ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves,
001:023 because, if any one is a hearer of the word and not a doer, this one hath been like to a man viewing his natural face in a mirror,
001:024 for he did view himself, and hath gone away, and immediately he did forget of what kind he was;
001:025 and he who did look into the perfect law—that of liberty, and did continue there, this one—not a forgetful hearer becoming, but a doer of work—this one shall be happy in his doing.
001:026 If any one doth think to be religious among you, not bridling his tongue, but deceiving his heart, of this one vain is the religion;
001:027 religion pure and undefiled with the God and Father is this, to look after orphans and widows in their tribulation—unspotted to keep himself from the world.
002:001 ¶ °{Chapter II. may be divided into six parts; v. 1–4 exhortation against respect of persons; v. 5–7 from the doings of God and of the rich; v. 8–13 also from the royal law; v. 14–19 faith without works unprofitable and dead; v. 20–23 contrasted with that of Abraham; v. 24–26 and of Rahab.} My brethren, hold not, in respect of persons,°{Lit., in acceptances of faces} the faith of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ,
002:002 for if there may come into your synagogue a man with gold ring, in gay raiment, and there may come in also a poor man in vile raiment,
002:003 and ye may look upon him bearing the gay raiment, and may say to him, “Thou—sit thou here well,” and to the poor man may say, “Thou—stand thou there, or, Sit thou here under my footstool,”—
002:004 ye did not judge fully°{Lit., diversely (or thoroughly)} in yourselves, and did become ill-reasoning judges.
002:005 Hearken, my brethren beloved, did not God choose the poor of this world, rich in faith, and heirs of the reign that He promised to those loving Him?
002:006 and ye did dishonour the poor one; do not the rich oppress you and themselves draw you to judgment-seats;
002:007 do they not themselves speak evil of the good name that was called upon you?
002:008 If, indeed, royal law ye complete, according to the Writing, “Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself,”—ye do well;
002:009 and if ye accept persons, sin ye do work, being convicted by the law as transgressors;
002:010 for whoever the whole law shall keep, and shall stumble in one point, he hath become guilty of all;
002:011 for He who is saying, “Thou mayest not commit adultery,” said also, “Thou mayest do no murder;” and if thou shalt not commit adultery, and shalt commit murder, thou hast become a transgressor of law;
002:012 so speak ye and so do, as about by a law of liberty to be judged,
002:013 for the judgment without kindness is to him not having done kindness, and exult doth kindness over judgment.
002:014 ¶ What is the profit, my brethren, if faith, any one may speak of having, and works he may not have? is that faith able to save him?
002:015 and if a brother or sister may be naked, and may be destitute of the daily food,
002:016 and any one of you may say to them, “Depart ye in peace, be warmed, and be filled,” and may not give to them the things needful for the body,°{Lit., the very-sweet (or soothing) things of the body} what is the profit?
002:017 so also the faith, if it may not have works, is dead by itself.
002:018 But say may some one, Thou hast faith, and I have works, shew me thy faith out of thy works, and I will shew thee out of my works my faith:
002:019 thou—thou dost believe that God is one; thou dost well, and the demons believe, and they shudder!
002:020 And dost thou wish to know, O vain man, that the faith apart from the works is dead?
002:021 Abraham our father—was not he declared righteous out of works, having brought up Isaac his son upon the altar?
002:022 dost thou see that the faith was working with his works, and out of the works the faith was perfected?
002:023 and fulfilled was the Writing that is saying, “And Abraham did believe°{remained stedfast to} God, and it was reckoned to him—to righteousness;” and, “Friend of God” he was called.
002:024 Ye see, then, that out of works is man declared righteous, and not out of faith only;
002:025 and in like manner also Rahab the harlot—was she not out of works declared righteous, having received the messengers, and by another way having sent forth?
002:026 for as the body apart from the spirit is dead, so also the faith apart from the works is dead.
003:001 ¶ °{Chapter III. may be divided into three parts; v. 1–6 warning against becoming teachers and talkative; v. 7–12 because of the power of the tongue; v. 13–18 heavenly and earthly wisdom.} Many teachers become not, my brethren, having known that greater judgment we shall receive,
003:002 for we all make many stumbles; if any one in word doth not stumble, this one is a perfect man, able to bridle also the whole body;
003:003 lo, the bits we put into the mouths of the horses for their obeying us, and their whole body we turn about;
003:004 lo, also the ships, being so great, and by fierce winds being driven, are led about by a very small helm, whithersoever the impulse of the helmsman°{Lit., straightener} doth counsel,
003:005 so also the tongue is a little member, and doth boast greatly; lo, a little fire how much wood it doth kindle!
003:006 and the tongue is a fire, the world of the unrighteousness, so the tongue is set in our members, which is spotting our whole body, and is setting on fire the course of nature,°{Lit., the running of the birth (origen, descent)} and is set on fire by the gehenna.
003:007 For every nature, both of beasts and of fowls, both of creeping things and things of the sea, is subdued, and hath been subdued, by the human nature,
003:008 and the tongue no one of men is able to subdue, it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison,
003:009 with it we do bless°{Lit., speak well of} the God and Father, and with it we do curse the men made according to the similitude of God;
003:010 out of the same mouth doth come forth blessing and cursing; it doth not need, my brethren, these things so to happen;
003:011 doth the fountain out of the same opening pour forth the sweet and the bitter?
003:012 is a fig-tree able, my brethren, olives to make? or a vine figs? so no fountain salt and sweet water is able to make.
003:013 ¶ Who is wise and intelligent among you? let him shew out of the good behaviour his works in meekness of wisdom,
003:014 and if bitter zeal ye have, and rivalry in your heart, glory not, nor lie against the truth;
003:015 this wisdom is not descending from above, but earthly, physical, demon-like,
003:016 for where zeal and rivalry are, there is insurrection°{Lit., un-settled-downness} and every evil matter;
003:017 and the wisdom from above, first, indeed, is pure, then peaceable, gentle, easily entreated, full of kindness and good fruits, uncontentious, and unhypocritical:—
003:018 and the fruit of the righteousness in peace is sown to those making peace.
004:001 °{Chapter IV. may be divided into four parts; v. 1–5 warning against wars and worldly friendship; v. 6–10 pride and impurity; v. 11, 12 judging a brother; v. 13–16 reckoning on the future.} ¶ Whence are wars and fightings among you? not thence—out of your passions, that are as soldiers in your members?
004:002 ye desire, and ye have not; ye murder,°{Or, kill (inwardly)} and are zealous, and are not able to attain; ye fight and war, and ye have not, because of your not asking;
004:003 ye ask, and ye receive not, because evilly ye ask, that in your pleasures ye may spend it.
004:004 Adulterers and adulteresses! have ye not known that friendship of the world is enmity with God? whoever, then, may counsel to be a friend of the world, an enemy of God he is set.
004:005 Do ye think that emptily the Writing saith, “To envy°{Or, withering} earnestly desireth the spirit that did dwell in us,”
004:006 and greater grace°{Lit., cause of leaping for joy} he doth give, wherefore he saith, “God against proud ones doth set Himself up, and to lowly ones He doth give grace?”
004:007 be subject, then, to God; stand up against the devil, and he will flee from you;
004:008 draw nigh to God, and He will draw nigh to you; cleanse hands, ye sinners! and purify hearts, ye two-souled!
004:009 be exceeding afflicted, and mourn, and weep, let your laughter to mourning be turned, and the joy to heaviness;°{Lit., a down-cast eye}
004:010 be made low before the Lord, and He shall exalt you.
004:011 ¶ Speak not one against another, brethren; he who is speaking against a brother, and is judging his brother, doth speak against law, and doth judge law, and if law thou dost judge, thou art not a doer of law but a judge;
004:012 one is the lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy; thou—who art thou that dost judge the other?
004:013 ¶ Go, now, ye who are saying, “To-day and to-morrow we will go on to such a city, and will pass there one year, and traffic, and make gain;”
004:014 who do not know the thing of the morrow; for what is your life? for it is a vapour that is appearing for a little, and then is vanishing;
004:015 instead of your saying, “If the Lord may will, we shall live, and do this or that;”
004:016 and now ye glory in your pride;°{Lit., assumptions} all such glorying is evil;
004:017 to him, then, knowing to do good, and not doing, sin it is to him.
005:001 °{Chapter V. may be divided into seven parts; v. 1–6 warnings for the rich; v. 7–9 exhortation to patience; v. 10, 11 from the example of Job; v. 12 against swearing; v. 13–15 directions for the suffering and the joyful; v. 16–18 mutual confessions and power of prayer; v. 19, 20 value of a straying one.} ¶ Go, now, ye rich! weep, howling over your miseries that are coming upon you;
005:002 your riches have rotted, and your garments have become moth-eaten;
005:003 your gold and silver have rotted, and the rust of them for a testimony shall be to you, and shall eat your flesh as fire. Ye made treasure in the last days!°{of Judaism}
005:004 lo, the reward of the workmen, of those who in-gathered your fields, which hath been fraudulently kept back°{Lit., un-confirmed} by you—doth cry out, and the exclamations of those who did reap into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth have entered;
005:005 ye did live in luxury upon the earth, and were wanton;°{Lit., adorned yourselves} ye did nourish your hearts, as in a day of slaughter;
005:006 ye did condemn—ye did murder the righteous one, he doth not resist you.
005:007 ¶ Be patient, then, brethren, till the presence of the Lord; lo, the husbandman doth expect the precious fruit of the earth, being patient for it, till he may receive rain—early and latter;
005:008 be patient, ye also; establish your hearts, because the presence of the Lord hath drawn nigh;°{the Roman armies beginning to encompass Jerusalem}
005:009 murmur not against°{Lit., be not constrained against} one another, brethren, that ye may not be condemned; lo, the Judge before the door hath stood.
005:010 An example take ye of the suffering of evil, my brethren, and of the patience, the prophets who did speak in the name of the Lord;
005:011 lo, we call happy those who are enduring; the endurance of Job ye heard of, and the end of the Lord ye have seen, that very compassionate°{Lit., has many bowels} is the Lord, and pitying.
005:012 ¶ And before all things, my brethren, do not swear, neither by the heaven, neither by the earth, neither by any other oath, and let your Yes be Yes, and the No, No; that under judgment ye may not fall.
005:013 ¶ Doth any one suffer evil among you? let him pray; is any of good cheer? let him sing psalms;°{play the psaltery}
005:014 is any infirm among you? let him call for the elders of the assembly, and let them pray over him, having anointed him with oil, in the name of the Lord,
005:015 and the prayer of the faith shall save the distressed one, and the Lord shall raise him up, and if sins he may have committed, they shall be forgiven to him.
005:016 Be confessing to one another the trespasses,°{Lit., fallings-aside} and be praying for one another, that ye may be healed; very strong is a working supplication of a righteous man;
005:017 Elijah was a man like affected as we, and with prayer he did pray—not to rain, and it did not rain upon the land three years and six months;
005:018 and again he did pray, and the heaven did give rain, and the land did bring forth her fruit.
005:019 Brethren, if any among you may go astray from the truth, and any one may turn him back,
005:020 let him know that he who did turn back a sinner from the straying of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall cover a multitude of sins.°{“missings” of the mark}
